# 1. Research Motivation

Artificial systems commonly begin with a predefined vocabulary of variables, features, objects, states, or latent dimensions.

They may then learn:

- relationships between variables;
- predictive models;
- causal structures;
- latent representations;
- policies;
- abstractions.

Project UNKNOWN investigates a preceding question:

> What happens when the system itself must determine that its current vocabulary is insufficient?

The project therefore focuses on representation discovery before relationship discovery.

---

# 2. Primary Research Question

> Can an artificial system autonomously detect when its current representation of an environment is systematically inadequate, construct a reusable candidate concept that extends that representation, and experimentally determine whether the candidate provides explanatory, predictive, interventional, and transfer value beyond the original representation?

---

# 3. Working Hypothesis

When an environment contains reusable structure that cannot be adequately represented by the system's current vocabulary, a system equipped with:

- representation-failure detection;
- candidate concept formation;
- hypothesis generation;
- prediction;
- intervention;
- falsification;
- transfer evaluation

may be able to construct and validate reusable abstractions that provide measurable value beyond the original representation.

This remains a hypothesis.

---

# 4. Null Hypothesis

Adding autonomous concept formation and validation does not produce reliable improvement over appropriately designed simpler baselines.

The baselines will include fixed-representation, statistical feature discovery, latent representation, and predictive approaches as appropriate.
