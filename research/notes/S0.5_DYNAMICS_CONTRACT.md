# S0.5 Dynamics Contract

**Project:** Project UNKNOWN
**Sprint:** S0.5 — Deterministic Benchmark Dynamics
**Stage:** S0.5-A — Dynamics Contract
**Status:** Draft for implementation review
**Date:** 2026-09-23

---

## 1. Purpose

This document defines the formal dynamics contract for the deterministic synthetic environment used by Project UNKNOWN.

The purpose of this contract is to establish an explicit, reproducible, scientifically controlled world in which later experiments can distinguish:

1. information-unavailable conditions;
2. representation-level failure;
3. model-family failure;
4. parameter failure.

The environment is not itself the discovery system.

It is the controlled experimental world against which a discovery system will eventually be evaluated.

This document therefore defines the environment independently of any future representation-discovery algorithm.

---

## 2. Scientific Role

The environment must support experiments of the following general form:

$$
S_t \xrightarrow{T,A,I} S_{t+1}
$$

with public observations produced by a projection:

$$
O_t = P(S_t)
$$

where:

* \(S_t\) is the complete world state;
* \(T\) is the transition mechanism;
* \(A\) is an ordinary agent action;
* \(I\) is an intervention;
* \(P\) is the public observation projection;
* \(O_t\) is the information available to the discovery system.

The discovery system must not receive the complete state \(S_t\).

The environment therefore deliberately separates:

```text
FULL WORLD STATE
       |
       | public projection
       v
PUBLIC OBSERVATION
       |
       v
DISCOVERY SYSTEM
```

Hidden benchmark truth and evaluation truth remain outside this public interface.

---

## 3. Research Boundary

The environment must support the following distinction.

### 3.1 Information Unavailable

A relevant distinction does not exist in the permitted public observation interface.

No representation transformation operating only on permitted observations should be able to recover the missing information.

Formally, if \(X\) is required information:

$$
X \notin \mathcal{I}(O_{\leq t})
$$

for the relevant information available through the public observation history.

This condition is a negative control.

---

### 3.2 Representation Failure

The required information is available through the permitted observation interface but is absent from the current representation.

Conceptually:

$$
X \in \mathcal{I}(O_{\leq t})
$$

but:

$$
X \notin R(O_{\leq t})
$$

for the current representation \(R\).

An allowed representation extension may recover the distinction:

$$
R' = R \oplus C
$$

This is the principal failure condition that later Project UNKNOWN experiments will investigate.

---

### 3.3 Model-Family Failure

The required information is available in the current representation, but the selected model family cannot express the relevant dependency.

The benchmark must permit a stronger model family to succeed without changing the underlying representation.

This prevents model expressiveness failure from being incorrectly attributed to representation failure.

---

### 3.4 Parameter Failure

The representation and model family are sufficient, but the selected parameters are inadequate.

Optimization or parameter recovery should be capable of resolving the failure without changing the representation.

This prevents ordinary parameter estimation failure from being incorrectly attributed to representation failure.

---

## 4. Formal Environment Definition

The environment is modeled as:

$$
E = (S,A,O,T,I)
$$

where:

### \(S\) — State

The complete internal state of the synthetic world.

### \(A\) — Actions

Ordinary operations available to the agent.

### \(O\) — Observations

Public information exposed to the agent.

### \(T\) — Transition

The deterministic state-transition mechanism.

### \(I\) — Interventions

Controlled modifications used for experimental testing.

The benchmark may additionally maintain hidden evaluation information:

$$
H
$$

and evaluation records:

$$
V
$$

but neither \(H\) nor \(V\) belongs to the public environment interface.

---

## 5. State Model

The initial world state is conceptually represented as:

$$
S_t = (E_t,R_t,C_t,Q_t)
$$

where:

* \(E_t\) = entities;
* \(R_t\) = relations;
* \(C_t\) = world context;
* \(Q_t\) = episode status.

The implementation may use a more precise internal representation provided that the public/hidden/evaluation boundary remains intact.

---

## 6. Entity Model

An entity represents a persistent object in the synthetic environment.

Conceptually:

$$
e_i = (id_i,p_i,v_i,m_i,c_i,\ldots)
$$

where:

* \(id_i\) = entity identifier;
* \(p_i\) = position;
* \(v_i\) = velocity;
* \(m_i\) = physical or behavioral property;
* \(c_i\) = category or context.

Not every state field is required to be publicly observable.

The benchmark must be able to construct conditions where relevant entity properties are:

* publicly observable;
* partially observable;
* represented indirectly;
* absent from the public interface.

Entity identifiers must not encode hidden benchmark truth.

For example, identifiers must not contain strings equivalent to:

```text
heavy-entity
target-entity
causal-entity
representation-failure
hidden-variable
```

Opaque identifiers are preferred.

---

## 7. Position

The initial environment uses a two-dimensional continuous world.

Conceptually:

$$
p_i=(x_i,y_i)
$$

Positions must remain within the configured world boundaries unless a future benchmark explicitly defines another boundary condition.

The public representation may expose position directly or through a controlled observation projection depending on the benchmark condition.

---

## 8. Velocity

Entities may possess a two-dimensional velocity:

$$
v_i=(v_{x_i},v_{y_i})
$$

The transition system may update position according to velocity.

A minimal deterministic transition may use:

$$
p_{t+1}=p_t+v_t\Delta t
$$

where \(\Delta t\) is fixed by the environment configuration.

The exact dynamics may later be extended, but the transition must remain deterministic for a fixed configuration, seed, action sequence, and intervention sequence.

---

## 9. Physical and Behavioral Properties

Entities may contain additional properties required to create benchmark conditions.

Examples include:

* mass-like quantities;
* friction-like quantities;
* category;
* interaction properties;
* context membership.

Such properties are part of the complete world state.

Their public availability must be controlled by the observation projection and benchmark condition.

The environment must never rely on property names or metadata as a hidden communication channel.

---

## 10. Relations

The environment supports relations between entities.

Conceptually:

$$
r_{ij}=R(e_i,e_j)
$$

Relations may represent structures such as:

* proximity;
* interaction;
* following;
* connection;
* membership;
* relative motion.

Relations are important because later representation extensions may need to express functions of multiple entities:

$$
C(e_i,e_j)
$$

rather than only unary properties:

$$
C(e_i)
$$

S0.5-A does not designate any particular relation as the expected discovery target.

The environment must remain capable of supporting multiple scientifically valid candidate relations.

---

## 11. World Context

The world may contain contextual variables that influence dynamics.

Examples include:

* environmental regime;
* interaction regime;
* scene configuration;
* benchmark-controlled context.

Context must be treated as world state rather than as an implicit benchmark label.

If context is hidden from the public interface, it must not be encoded indirectly through:

* identifiers;
* filenames;
* metadata;
* ordering;
* error messages;
* observation counts;
* timing behavior;
* directory names.

---

## 12. Episode State

Every environment episode has an explicit lifecycle:

```text
UNINITIALIZED
      |
      v
   ACTIVE
      |
      v
  TERMINAL
```

### UNINITIALIZED

No valid episode exists.

Operations requiring an active environment must fail explicitly.

### ACTIVE

The environment can accept valid actions and interventions.

### TERMINAL

The episode has reached its configured termination condition.

Further state-changing operations must fail explicitly.

The environment must never silently restart or continue a terminal episode.

---

## 13. Reset Contract

The public runtime provides:

```text
reset(config, seed)
```

Reset must:

1. initialize a new episode;
2. apply the supplied configuration;
3. establish deterministic initial state;
4. establish the episode lifecycle;
5. return the initial public observation.

The reset operation must not expose hidden benchmark truth.

The public response must not reveal:

* benchmark condition;
* latent variable assignments;
* causal structure;
* evaluation labels;
* ground-truth representation;
* hidden evaluator identifiers.

---

## 14. Determinism Contract

Determinism is a hard requirement.

For a fixed:

$$
(config,seed,A_{1:n},I_{1:m})
$$

the resulting trajectory must be reproducible.

Therefore:

```text
same configuration
+
same seed
+
same actions
+
same interventions
=
same trajectory
```

The implementation must not rely on uncontrolled sources of nondeterminism.

This includes avoiding dependence on:

* wall-clock time;
* process-specific randomness;
* unordered iteration where ordering affects state;
* external network state;
* external services;
* machine-local hidden state.

Different seeds may produce different valid episodes.

Determinism does not require all seeds to produce the same world.

---

## 15. Action Model

The public environment supports the following initial action kinds.

### 15.1 NO_OP

The agent performs no direct movement or interaction.

The environment may still advance according to deterministic world dynamics.

### 15.2 MOVE

The agent supplies a movement vector.

The environment applies that action according to the configured transition rules.

A MOVE action without a required vector is invalid.

### 15.3 INTERACT

The agent attempts an interaction with an entity or relation.

The exact interaction semantics will be implemented by the dynamics layer.

An interaction must not silently mutate unrelated hidden state.

---

## 16. Action Contract

Actions must be:

1. explicitly represented;
2. validated against the environment configuration;
3. deterministic;
4. included in reproducibility records;
5. independent of hidden benchmark labels.

Invalid actions must raise explicit runtime errors.

The environment must not reinterpret an invalid action as another action.

---

## 17. Intervention Model

Interventions are distinct from ordinary actions.

An action represents normal interaction with the environment.

An intervention represents a controlled experimental modification used to evaluate a hypothesis.

Initial public intervention kinds are:

### SET_POSITION

Set an entity's position to a specified public vector.

### SET_VELOCITY

Set an entity's velocity to a specified public vector.

### REMOVE_ENTITY

Remove a selected entity from the active world.

Interventions must be deterministic and explicitly represented.

---

## 18. Intervention Semantics

An intervention should be interpreted as a controlled modification:

$$
do(X=x)
$$

rather than as an ordinary learned policy action.

The environment must record the intervention as part of the reproducibility state.

The intervention interface must not reveal:

* why the intervention was selected;
* which hidden variable is scientifically relevant;
* the expected evaluation outcome;
* the benchmark condition.

Intervention behavior may change future observations, but it must do so through explicit deterministic transition semantics.

---

## 19. Transition Contract

The transition function is:

$$
S_{t+1}=T(S_t,A_t,I_t)
$$

where an ordinary step may omit intervention:

$$
S_{t+1}=T(S_t,A_t)
$$

The transition function must be:

* deterministic;
* explicit;
* testable;
* reproducible;
* independent of evaluator state.

The transition mechanism must not access evaluation labels to decide world behavior.

Hidden benchmark configuration may determine which world is constructed, but the resulting dynamics must behave according to the world definition rather than evaluator-side scoring logic.

---

## 20. Time

The initial environment uses discrete simulation steps.

Let:

$$
t \in \{0,1,2,\ldots,T\}
$$

Each successful state-changing operation advances the simulation according to the defined transition semantics.

The initial implementation must not use wall-clock time as simulation time.

Therefore:

$$
simulation\ time \neq wall\ clock\ time
$$

This prevents timing from becoming an unintended source of information leakage.

---

## 21. Terminal Conditions

An episode terminates when its configured maximum step count is reached or when a future benchmark-specific terminal condition explicitly applies.

The terminal condition must be deterministic.

After termination:

```text
step()
```

and:

```text
intervene()
```

must fail explicitly.

Observation of the final state remains valid where defined by the public runtime contract.

---

## 22. Public Observation Projection

The public observation is generated by:

$$
O_t=P(S_t)
$$

The projection is the principal information boundary between the complete world and the discovery system.

The projection may expose:

* public entity identifiers;
* public positions;
* public velocities where configured;
* public categories where configured;
* public relations where configured;
* public events.

The projection must not expose:

* hidden variables;
* ground-truth causal factors;
* benchmark condition labels;
* evaluator records;
* future states;
* counterfactual outcomes;
* transfer-set truth;
* hidden candidate representations.

---

## 23. Observation History

A discovery system may require temporal information.

The environment therefore permits observations to be consumed as a sequence:

$$
O_{\leq t}=(O_0,O_1,\ldots,O_t)
$$

The environment must distinguish:

* current observation;
* previous observations;
* future observations.

Future observations must never be exposed before they occur.

The environment must not encode hidden truth through observation-history length beyond what naturally follows from the public episode trajectory.

---

## 24. Public Metadata

Operational metadata may be exposed when required for normal environment operation.

Examples include:

* environment version;
* schema version;
* configured maximum steps;
* allowed public action kinds;
* allowed public intervention kinds.

Metadata must not expose benchmark semantics.

Forbidden metadata includes:

```text
condition_kind
ground_truth_variable
causal_graph
hidden_state
evaluation_label
transfer_label
expected_concept
```

---

## 25. Hidden Benchmark State

Hidden benchmark state exists outside the public runtime boundary.

It may contain:

* benchmark condition;
* complete latent state;
* true causal dependencies;
* ground-truth representation;
* hidden variable assignments;
* paired-condition identifiers;
* benchmark construction metadata.

Hidden benchmark state is available only to controlled benchmark and evaluation components.

It must never be serialized into public observations.

---

## 26. Evaluation State

Evaluation state is separate from both the public runtime and hidden benchmark construction state.

Evaluation may contain:

* prediction truth;
* intervention outcomes;
* falsification records;
* transfer truth;
* representation recovery information;
* complexity measurements;
* discovery outcome labels.

Evaluation information is not an environment observation.

The discovery system must not access evaluation records during discovery.

---

## 27. Paired Benchmark Principle

The benchmark must support paired conditions that differ in a controlled causal or representational property while holding unrelated factors as constant as practical.

The purpose is to distinguish failure mechanisms.

Examples include:

### Model-family pair

```text
same relevant information
same representation
weak model fails
stronger model succeeds
```

### Representation pair

```text
same underlying world
relevant distinction unavailable to R
relevant distinction available to R'
```

### Information-unavailable control

```text
relevant distinction absent from public information
```

### Parameter control

```text
representation sufficient
model family sufficient
parameters insufficient
```

These paired conditions must be generated independently of the discovery system's conclusions.

---

## 28. Representation Neutrality

The environment must not assume that one particular candidate representation is the correct answer to the research problem.

The benchmark may have known ground truth for evaluation, but the public environment must not instruct the discovery system which representation to construct.

The evaluator may determine whether a candidate is equivalent, approximately equivalent, predictively useful, or unsupported.

The environment itself must remain agnostic during discovery.

---

## 29. Leakage Prevention

The following are explicitly prohibited as public information channels:

### Direct leakage

* hidden variables;
* benchmark labels;
* evaluation labels;
* causal graphs;
* ground-truth candidate expressions.

### Identifier leakage

* semantic entity names;
* condition names;
* variable names;
* benchmark-specific codes.

### Ordering leakage

* arranging observations according to hidden class;
* deterministic ordering that encodes latent truth.

### Metadata leakage

* hidden condition in metadata;
* hidden variable names;
* evaluator identifiers.

### Filesystem leakage

* benchmark labels in filenames;
* condition names in public directories;
* hidden truth in import paths.

### Serialization leakage

* hidden state embedded in public objects;
* hidden fields retained during serialization.

### Logging leakage

* hidden truth in debug logs;
* evaluator labels in exception messages.

### Timing leakage

* wall-clock delays associated with hidden conditions;
* different execution timing deliberately encoding benchmark state.

### Randomness leakage

* exposing the seed as a proxy for benchmark condition when seed assignment carries hidden semantics.

---

## 30. Error Contract

Errors are part of the public interface and therefore must be treated as potential information channels.

Public errors may explain invalid public operations.

They must not reveal:

* hidden benchmark state;
* evaluator truth;
* unavailable variables;
* causal dependencies;
* expected candidate concepts.

For example, an invalid action may produce:

```text
MOVE actions require a public vector.
```

but must not produce:

```text
MOVE rejected because hidden variable MASS is unavailable.
```

---

## 31. External Dependency Constraint

The initial deterministic benchmark must not depend on:

* network services;
* external APIs;
* remote model providers;
* real-time external data;
* machine-specific external state.

The benchmark must be executable locally and reproducibly.

External tools may later assist analysis, but they must not define the benchmark's scientific ground truth.

---

## 32. Configuration Contract

A public environment configuration must contain only information necessary to construct and operate the public environment.

Configuration may include:

* world dimensions;
* entity count;
* maximum steps;
* environment version;
* schema version;
* public action configuration;
* public intervention configuration;
* observation history limit.

Benchmark truth must not be embedded in public configuration.

If a benchmark runner needs hidden configuration, it must remain outside the public runtime boundary.

---

## 33. Seed Contract

The seed is a reproducibility input.

A seed must:

1. be explicitly accepted by environment initialization;
2. deterministically influence any permitted stochastic initialization;
3. not itself encode benchmark condition semantics;
4. be sufficient to reproduce a generated episode when combined with configuration and actions.

The benchmark must avoid assigning semantic meaning to specific seed values that could become an information channel.

---

## 34. Reproducibility Record

A reproducible experiment must be identifiable by the combination of:

$$
(config,seed,A_{1:n},I_{1:m})
$$

plus the environment implementation version.

A future experiment runner should therefore be able to reconstruct the public trajectory without access to evaluator state.

---

## 35. Scientific Independence of Evaluation

The environment transition system must not query the evaluator to determine whether a state transition is successful.

The causal direction is:

```text
environment
     |
     v
public observations
     |
     v
discovery system
     |
     v
candidate / hypothesis
     |
     v
evaluation
```

not:

```text
environment
     |
     +----> evaluator
     |
     +----> tells discovery system what is correct
```

The evaluator assesses outcomes after or during controlled testing without leaking its truth labels into discovery.

---

## 36. Required Future Benchmark Capabilities

The S0.5 dynamics implementation must leave room for:

* multiple entities;
* temporal dynamics;
* relational interactions;
* controlled interventions;
* information-unavailable controls;
* representation-failure controls;
* model-family controls;
* parameter-failure controls;
* decoy structures;
* paired counterfactual conditions;
* unseen-context transfer;
* ground-truth evaluation;
* deterministic replay.

These capabilities do not all need to be fully implemented in the first minimal world.

The architecture must avoid preventing them.

---

## 37. What S0.5-A Does Not Claim

Completion of this contract does **not** demonstrate:

* autonomous concept discovery;
* representation failure detection;
* representation extension;
* causal discovery;
* causal correctness;
* predictive superiority;
* intervention validity;
* transfer validity;
* scientific novelty;
* superiority over existing methods.

S0.5-A establishes only the environment contract required to test those questions later.

---

## 38. Implementation Constraints

The eventual implementation must:

1. preserve the S0.4 public/hidden/evaluation boundary;
2. use explicit typed schemas where appropriate;
3. remain deterministic;
4. remain locally reproducible;
5. expose no benchmark truth through public objects;
6. expose no benchmark truth through metadata;
7. expose no benchmark truth through errors;
8. expose no benchmark truth through filesystem structure;
9. expose no benchmark truth through logs;
10. maintain tests for every boundary-sensitive behavior.

Implementation should favor small, composable deterministic components over a monolithic simulator.

---

## 39. Testing Requirements

The S0.5 implementation must eventually test at least:

### State tests

* valid initial state;
* entity creation;
* entity removal;
* position updates;
* velocity updates;
* relation updates.

### Action tests

* valid NO_OP;
* valid MOVE;
* valid INTERACT;
* invalid action rejection.

### Intervention tests

* SET_POSITION;
* SET_VELOCITY;
* REMOVE_ENTITY;
* invalid intervention rejection.

### Lifecycle tests

* operation before reset;
* normal episode progression;
* terminal behavior;
* operation after termination.

### Determinism tests

* same seed and config;
* same action sequence;
* same intervention sequence;
* same complete trajectory.

### Isolation tests

* public observation contains no hidden fields;
* public metadata contains no hidden labels;
* errors contain no hidden truth;
* serialization contains no hidden truth.

### Scientific condition tests

* information-unavailable configuration;
* representation-failure configuration;
* model-family-failure configuration;
* parameter-failure configuration.

The last category should verify benchmark construction and hidden ground truth only through trusted internal tests, never through the public discovery interface.

## 43. Contract Principle

The governing principle for S0.5 is:

> **Build a world whose failures can be interpreted before building a system that tries to discover them.**

A benchmark that cannot distinguish its own failure modes cannot provide strong evidence about representation discovery.

Therefore:

$$
\text{Controlled World}
\rightarrow
\text{Controlled Failure}
\rightarrow
\text{Controlled Experiment}
\rightarrow
\text{Scientific Evidence}
$$

This is the foundation of S0.5.
