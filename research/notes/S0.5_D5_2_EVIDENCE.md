# S0.5-D.5.2 Evidence — World-Family Design

**Project:** Project UNKNOWN
**Sprint:** S0 — Scientific Foundation
**Stage:** S0.5 — Executable Benchmark Specification
**Decision:** D.5.2 — World-Family Design
**Status:** COMPLETE — Research Specification
**Date:** 2026-09-24

---

## 1. Objective

D.5.2 defines the world-family architecture required to turn the controlled benchmark contract from D.4 into an executable scientific environment.

The objective is to establish a shared world-generation framework capable of constructing:

- R0 — Representation-Sufficient Control
- P1 — Parameter Failure
- M1 — Model-Family Failure
- R1 — Representation-Level Inadequacy
- N1 — Non-Identifiable / Insufficient Evidence
- X1 — Noise / Stochastic Variation
- T1 — Unseen-Context Transfer

without implementing separate condition-specific environments that expose their identity through implementation artifacts.

The central design requirement is:

> Benchmark conditions must be generated from shared world-family machinery and differ through explicitly controlled structural factors.

This prevents the benchmark from reducing to a collection of unrelated toy environments.

---

## 2. Core Research Decision

D.5.2 adopts a **typed relational dynamical world** as the core benchmark family.

The world is conceptually represented as:

```text
W = (E, X, R, F, G, I, ξ)
```

where:

- `E` = entities
- `X` = observable and hidden state
- `R` = relations
- `F` = transition dynamics
- `G` = observation function
- `I` = intervention transition system
- `ξ` = stochastic/noise process

The benchmark condition is not a separate environment implementation.

Instead:

```text
Shared World Family
        ↓
Controlled Configuration
        ↓
World Instance
        ↓
Benchmark Condition
```

This provides a common scientific substrate for comparing failure classes.

---

## 3. Why a Shared World Family Is Required

A benchmark composed of unrelated environments could make failure-class comparisons ambiguous.

For example:

- an R1 world might differ from an R0 world in entity count;
- an M1 world might have different timing;
- a P1 world might use different noise;
- an N1 world might expose different interventions.

UNKNOWN could then classify conditions using superficial statistics rather than discovering representation inadequacy.

A shared family allows the benchmark to hold irrelevant factors constant or overlapping while deliberately changing the scientific factor of interest.

Therefore:

> Condition identity must be a property of the generated mechanism and representation relationship, not a property of the environment's implementation identity.

## 4. Core World Ontology

The world family contains:

```text
World
├── Entities
├── Observable State
├── Hidden State
├── Relations
├── Dynamics

├── Intervention Transition Rules
├── Observation Function
```

The ontology is intentionally generic.

It must support different mechanisms without introducing semantic labels that reveal the benchmark condition.

## 5. Entity Model

The initial entity abstraction is:

```text
Entity
├── id
├── position
├── velocity
├── existence
└── observable attributes
```

Entity identifiers are neutral.

Examples:

- `entity_0`
- `entity_1`
- `entity_2`
- `entity_3`

Semantic identifiers such as:

- `predator`
- `prey`
- `leader`
- `follower`
- `cause`
- `target`

are prohibited unless that semantic information is genuinely part of the public environment.

This prevents identity leakage from becoming a shortcut for failure classification.

## 6. Observable State

Public observations may contain information explicitly allowed by the benchmark contract.

Candidate observable information includes:

- entity position
- entity velocity
- entity existence
- observable scalar attributes
- observable categorical attributes
- observable relations
- step index
- ordinary action outcomes
- intervention outcomes

Public observation must not contain evaluator interpretation.

In particular, UNKNOWN must not receive:

- `condition_id`
- `failure_class`
- `representation_sufficient`
- `model_family_sufficient`
- `parameter_status`
- `missing_concept`
- `recommended_extension`
- `ground_truth_mechanism`
- `hidden_state`
- `evaluator_diagnosis`

---

## 7. Hidden State

Hidden state may include:

- latent regime
- internal mechanism state
- hidden interaction state
- hidden parameters
- unobservable relations
- generator state
- private mechanism identifiers

However:

> Hidden state is not automatically representation inadequacy.

A hidden variable only supports an R1 construction when the relevant structure is recoverable from the permitted public interaction boundary and the baseline representation cannot express the required distinction.

Otherwise the condition may be N1 or simply an environment with hidden state.

This distinction is essential.

---

## 8. Dynamics

World evolution is conceptually represented as:

```text
S_(t+1) = F(S_t, A_t, I_t, θ, ξ_t)
```

where:

- `S_t` = complete world state
- `A_t` = ordinary action
- `I_t` = intervention
- `θ` = mechanism parameters
- `ξ_t` = stochastic variation

Public observation is:

```text
O_t = G(S_t)
```

UNKNOWN receives `O_t`, not the complete `S_t`.

The evaluator may retain `S_t` privately for benchmark verification.

---

## 9. Ordinary Actions and Interventions

Ordinary actions and interventions remain scientifically distinct.

### Ordinary action

An ordinary action is part of normal environment interaction.

Examples include:

- `NO_OP`
- `MOVE`
- `INTERACT`

### Intervention

An intervention deliberately modifies a selected part of the world according to the benchmark's intervention contract.

Examples include:

- `SET_POSITION`
- `SET_VELOCITY`
- `REMOVE_ENTITY`

The benchmark must preserve this distinction because intervention outcomes are evidence about mechanism structure rather than merely another ordinary action.

---

## 10. Public Interaction Contract

The benchmark environment exposes only the public interaction boundary.

Conceptually:

```text
UNKNOWN
   │
   ├── observe()
   │
   ├── act(...)
   │
   └── intervene(...)
```

The environment may internally maintain:

- `PRIVATE WORLD STATE`
- `PRIVATE GROUND TRUTH`
- `PRIVATE CONDITION CLASS`
- `PRIVATE EVALUATOR RECORD`

but these are never returned to UNKNOWN.

The public runtime remains the authoritative source of observations and transition outcomes.

---

## 11. R0 — Representation-Sufficient Control

R0 represents the baseline control condition.

Requirements:

- R₀ is sufficient to express the relevant structure.
- The selected model family is sufficient.
- Correct parameters exist.
- The world is learnable under the permitted protocol.
- No representation extension is required.

R0 is therefore the negative control for representation invention.

A correct system should not interpret ordinary learnable error in R0 as evidence that a new representation is required.

---

## 12. P1 — Parameter-Failure Construction

P1 isolates parameter failure.

Requirements:

- R₀ sufficient
- M₀ sufficient
- θ_current incorrect
- θ* exists

The representation remains capable of expressing the relevant structure.

The selected model family remains capable of representing the correct dynamics.

The failure is caused by incorrect parameter values.

Therefore the correct recovery path is parameter updating.

A representation extension is not required.

This condition tests whether UNKNOWN over-triggers concept formation whenever prediction error occurs.

---

## 13. M1 — Model-Family-Failure Construction

M1 isolates model-family failure.

Requirements:

- R₀ sufficient
- M₀ insufficient
- M₁ sufficient

The representation preserves the information required to solve the task.

The selected model family cannot express the required mapping.

A richer or otherwise appropriate model family can solve the task without changing the representation.

Therefore:

> Model-family failure must remain downstream of the fixed R₀ representation boundary.

A model-family upgrade that bypasses R₀ would invalidate the benchmark distinction.

---

## 14. R1 — Representation-Level Inadequacy

R1 is the central benchmark construction.

A clean R1 requires:

- R₀ cannot express required structure
- R₀ parameter changes cannot repair it
- M₀ cannot repair it
- permitted M-family expansion cannot repair it
- valid R₁ extension can express it
- public evidence can support discovering the distinction

R1 is not defined as:

> "A candidate representation improved performance."

That would be circular.

Instead, R1 must be constructed structurally before UNKNOWN is evaluated.

---

## 15. Constructive Representation Collision

The strongest R1 witness is a representation collision.

Let:

- `H₁` = public history 1
- `H₂` = public history 2

If:

```text
R₀(H₁) = R₀(H₂)
```

but:

```text
Y(H₁) ≠ Y(H₂)
```

then the baseline representation has collapsed two scientifically relevant states into the same representation.

A valid extension must satisfy:

```text
R₁(H₁) ≠ R₁(H₂)
```

and restore a predictive or explanatory distinction.

This establishes representation-level inadequacy independently of model fitting.

---

## 16. Why the Collision Is Important

Define:

```text
H₁ ~₀ H₂
iff
R₀(H₁) = R₀(H₂)
```

The baseline representation partitions histories into equivalence classes.

If two histories are in the same class but require different outcomes, then no downstream deterministic model operating only on R₀ can distinguish them.

This remains true even if the downstream model is highly expressive.

Therefore the failure is not automatically:

- insufficient optimization,
- insufficient parameters,
- insufficient neural capacity,
- insufficient training time.

It is an information-preservation problem induced by the representation boundary.

---

## 17. R1 Recovery Ladder

The benchmark must evaluate progressively stronger recovery mechanisms.

- **Level 0** — Baseline representation + baseline model
- **Level 1** — Baseline representation + parameter update
- **Level 2** — Baseline representation + permitted model-family expansion
- **Level 3** — Representation extension + suitable downstream model

A clean R1 should exhibit:

```text
Level 0  → failure
Level 1  → failure
Level 2  → failure
Level 3  → recovery
```

This ladder is one of the strongest controls against confusing representation failure with ordinary model failure.

---

## 18. R1 Public Recoverability

A hidden distinction is insufficient to establish R1.

The relevant structure must be recoverable through the public interaction boundary.

Therefore a valid R1 must satisfy:

- the relevant evidence is publicly observable;
- the evidence can be collected under the permitted protocol;
- the distinction is not directly disclosed by evaluator metadata;
- an extension can be constructed from public evidence;
- the extension can be experimentally tested.

If the distinction cannot be identified from public evidence, the condition may instead be N1.

---

## 19. Candidate R1 Structure

A higher-order relational structure remains a useful candidate construction.

However:

> Relation arity alone is not representation novelty.

If R₀ already permits arbitrary relational composition, a ternary relation may be reconstructible without a new representation.

Therefore the scientific object is:

> A bounded relational abstraction whose required distinctions are outside the declared R₀ expressibility class.

A concrete implementation may use a higher-order relational motif, but only after the R₀ grammar and composition limits are explicitly defined.

---

## 20. R0 Closure Requirement

Before R1 can be implemented, R₀ must have executable semantics.

Required future definitions include:

- primitive vocabulary
- primitive types
- operator signatures
- composition rules
- maximum composition depth where justified
- temporal history boundary
- representation serialization
- canonical representation form
- equality semantics
- structural equivalence semantics
- extension complexity

Without these definitions, the statement:

> R₁ is outside Expressible(R₀)

cannot be mechanically evaluated.

Therefore R0 closure is a dependency for later executable R1 construction.

---

## 21. N1 — Non-Identifiable / Insufficient Evidence

N1 represents genuine scientific uncertainty.

Requirements:

- multiple materially different explanations
- remain compatible with public evidence
- under the permitted experimental protocol

N1 must not simply be a hidden-variable condition.

If the evidence cannot distinguish between two explanations, UNKNOWN should be allowed to remain uncertain.

This is necessary to prevent forced concept formation.

---

## 22. X1 — Noise / Stochastic Variation

X1 isolates stochastic variation.

Requirements:

- representation sufficient
- model family sufficient
- parameters correct or learnable
- mechanism unchanged
- stochastic variation produces occasional errors

The benchmark must ensure that X1 does not systematically encode a representation-level distinction.

This tests whether UNKNOWN mistakes random prediction error for structural inadequacy.

---

## 23. T1 — Unseen-Context Transfer

T1 is a transfer evaluation condition rather than a failure class.

Its purpose is to determine whether an accepted representation extension captures reusable structure.

A candidate extension should be evaluated in contexts that were not used to construct or accept it.

The key question is:

> Does the extension represent a reusable abstraction, or did it merely patch the original environment?

Transfer must therefore involve structural novelty rather than only new random seeds.

---

## 24. Core Family and Held-Out Family

One core world family is sufficient for controlled construction of:

- R0
- P1
- M1
- R1
- N1
- X1

However, broad claims about reusable representation discovery require a structurally distinct held-out validation family.

Therefore:

```text
Core Family
    ↓
Failure Construction

Held-Out Family
    ↓
Transfer Validation
```

The held-out family must preserve the public interaction interface while changing relevant structural organization.

---

## 25. Structural Variation

Structural variation should include factors such as:

- mechanism composition
- relational topology
- interaction configuration
- context variables
- entity arrangements
- dynamic dependencies
- mechanism parameterization
- intervention targets

The benchmark should avoid using superficial changes as the primary source of novelty.

For example, changing:

```text
entity_0
```

to:

```text
entity_7
```

is not structural novelty.

---

## 26. Matched Worlds

Matched worlds are required for clean causal comparisons.

A matched experiment should hold constant:

- world family
- configuration
- initial state
- irrelevant factors
- seed hierarchy where appropriate

while changing only the declared experimental factor.

For example:

```text
Matched World A
Matched World B
       ↓
one controlled structural difference
```

This reduces initialization confounding.

---

## 27. Anti-Shortcut Requirements

The world generator must prevent condition classification from being based on superficial statistics.

Required controls include:

- neutral entity identifiers
- randomized irrelevant identity/order
- overlapping entity counts
- overlapping timing distributions
- overlapping noise characteristics where scientifically appropriate
- overlapping intervention availability
- matched initial-state distributions
- evaluator isolation
- structural train/dev/evaluation separation

Conditions must not have unique metadata fingerprints.

---

## 28. Condition Assignment Must Be Private

UNKNOWN must not receive:

- `condition_id`
- `failure_class`
- `representation_status`
- `model_family_status`
- `parameter_status`
- `extension_identifier`

The evaluator may maintain these privately.

The architecture is:

```text
PRIVATE BENCHMARK TRUTH
        ↓
    EVALUATOR
        ↓ observes
PUBLIC ENVIRONMENT
        ↓
     UNKNOWN
```

There must be no convenience API allowing UNKNOWN to query evaluator truth.

---

## 29. Intervention Compatibility

All relevant world conditions must support the intervention contract consistently enough that intervention availability does not reveal condition identity.

The intervention interface remains:

- `SET_POSITION`
- `SET_VELOCITY`
- `REMOVE_ENTITY`

where applicable.

The benchmark must distinguish:

- independent matched trials
- sequential trials

because intervention effects may accumulate in sequential experiments.

Counterfactual comparisons require matched initial states or formally equivalent initial conditions.

---

## 30. R1 Intervention Witness

Interventions can strengthen an R1 claim.

Suppose:

```text
R₀(H₁) = R₀(H₂)
```

but a controlled intervention produces:

```text
Y_intervention(H₁) ≠ Y_intervention(H₂)
```

while an R1 extension predicts the difference.

This provides interventional evidence that the collapsed distinction has causal consequences.

However:

> Intervention evidence strengthens an R1 witness; it does not define R1 by itself.

The underlying representation collision remains the primary constructive condition.

Required levels:

- **R0** — world reproducibility
- **R1** — trajectory reproducibility
- **R2** — intervention reproducibility
- **R3** — scenario reproducibility
- **R4** — evaluation reproducibility

The experiment identity should contain:

- `experiment_id`
- `condition_id`
- `scenario_id`
- `trial_id`
- `seed`
- `configuration_id`

UNKNOWN normally does not receive the seed.

## 32. Seed Hierarchy

The conceptual seed hierarchy is:

```text
Master Seed
├── World Seed
├── Scenario Seed
├── Trial Seed
├── Noise Seed
└── Evaluation Seed
```

Agent randomness must remain separate from environment/evaluator randomness.

Matched trials must differ only in declared experimental factors.

This prevents accidental coupling between random initialization and scientific conditions.

---

## 33. Deterministic R1 Construction

A constructive R1 should be deterministic given:

- benchmark version
- world configuration
- seed
- initial state
- condition configuration

This allows the evaluator to reproduce:

- representation collisions
- required outcomes
- intervention consequences
- recovery-ladder results
- transfer behavior

without relying on accidental stochastic behavior.

---

## 34. World-Family Independence

The core benchmark family must be defined independently of UNKNOWN's implementation.

The environment cannot be designed around the exact algorithm UNKNOWN happens to use.

Otherwise the benchmark risks becoming an architecture-specific test rather than a scientific test of representation discovery.

The benchmark contract must therefore precede final UNKNOWN implementation decisions.

---

## 35. Scientific Invariants

The following invariants are established:

**Invariant 1 — Shared Machinery**
Conditions are generated from common world-family machinery.

**Invariant 2 — Public/Private Separation**
UNKNOWN receives public environmental evidence only.

**Invariant 3 — Fixed R0 Boundary**
R0 is fixed before evaluation.

**Invariant 4 — Downstream Model Restriction**
Model-family expansion cannot bypass R0.

**Invariant 5 — Constructive R1**
R1 is defined through structural representation inadequacy, not performance improvement alone.

**Invariant 6 — Public Recoverability**
R1 distinctions must be recoverable through permitted public evidence.

**Invariant 7 — Non-Circular Evaluation**
Improvement is evidence for a predefined R1 construction, not its definition.

**Invariant 8 — Negative Controls**
P1, M1, and X1 test over-triggering.

**Invariant 9 — Uncertainty**
N1 permits UNKNOWN to remain uncertain.

**Invariant 10 — Transfer**
Accepted extensions require evaluation beyond their construction context.

## 38. R1 Candidate Acceptance Requirements

Before an R1 construction becomes an executable benchmark condition, it must satisfy:

1. R₀ is frozen.
2. R₀ semantics are executable.
3. A representation collision exists.
4. The required outcome differs across the collision.
5. The distinction is publicly recoverable.
6. Parameter updates cannot resolve the collision.
7. Permitted model-family expansion cannot resolve the collision.
8. A valid extension separates the collision.
9. The extension produces testable predictions.
10. Intervention evidence can be generated where appropriate.
11. Falsification conditions can be defined.
12. The extension can be evaluated under unseen structural context.
13. Candidate complexity can be measured.
14. Candidate provenance can be tracked.
15. Acceptance/rejection/stopping policy is predefined.

These requirements connect D.5.2 to the remaining D.5 stages.

## 41. Important Rejection

The following construction was explicitly rejected:

> "Add a ternary relation to the representation and call it a discovered concept."

**Reason:**

A ternary relation may already be expressible through existing pairwise relations and unrestricted composition.

Therefore:

```text
new arity ≠ new representation
```

The valid scientific object is a structure outside the declared expressibility class of R0.

```text
parameter problem
        vs
model-family problem
        vs
representation problem
        vs
insufficient evidence
        vs
```
