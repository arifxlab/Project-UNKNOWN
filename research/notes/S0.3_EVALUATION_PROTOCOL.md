# Project UNKNOWN — S0.3 Evaluation Protocol

**Status:** Working scientific specification
**Sprint:** Sprint 0 — S0.3
**Date:** 2026-09-23
**Purpose:** Define how Project UNKNOWN will distinguish representation-level inadequacy from model failure and evaluate candidate representations without benchmark leakage.

---

# 1. Objective

The evaluation protocol tests the central UNKNOWN hypothesis:

> An artificial system can potentially distinguish representation-level inadequacy from parameter and model-family failure, trigger constrained representation extension, and validate the resulting candidate representation as a reusable abstraction.

The protocol must be capable of falsifying this hypothesis.

---

# 2. Evaluation Principle

UNKNOWN must never be evaluated only by whether it eventually obtains a better predictive score.

The evaluation must separately measure:

1. failure diagnosis;
2. candidate construction;
3. predictive value;
4. intervention value;
5. falsification behavior;
6. transfer;
7. search efficiency;
8. reproducibility.

---

# 3. Dataset / Environment Separation

The experimental lifecycle is divided into:

```text
Discovery
    ↓
Candidate generation
    ↓
Internal validation
    ↓
Frozen candidate
    ↓
Independent evaluation
    ↓
Transfer evaluation
```

Discovery information must not leak into final validation.

---

# 4. Required Environment Splits

Each benchmark instance should, where feasible, provide:

## 4.1 Discovery environment

Used for:

* observing systematic failures;
* generating hypotheses;
* constructing candidate representations.

## 4.2 Validation environment

Used for:

* selecting among candidates;
* checking preliminary scientific consequences.

## 4.3 Intervention environment

Used for controlled interventions.

## 4.4 Transfer environments

Previously unseen environments used to test reuse.

## 4.5 Final evaluation environment

Used only after candidate construction and selection have been frozen.

---

# 5. Failure-Level Benchmark

The benchmark must contain controlled cases representing different failure causes.

## 5.1 Parameter Failure

Properties:

* current representation is sufficient;
* model family is sufficient;
* initial parameter state is inadequate.

Expected behavior:

```text
optimize parameters
```

Representation extension should not be required.

---

## 5.2 Model-Family Failure

Properties:

* current representation contains the required information;
* initial model family is insufficient;
* a predefined stronger model family can solve the task.

Expected behavior:

```text
change model family
```

Representation extension should not be required.

---

## 5.3 Representation Failure

Properties:

* current representation lacks a required distinction;
* predefined model-family alternatives cannot meet the adequacy criterion;
* admissible representation extension can express the missing distinction.

Expected behavior:

```text
propose representation extension
```

---

## 5.4 Language Insufficiency

Properties:

* required distinction cannot be expressed within the current extension language.

Expected behavior:

```text
report language/search limitation
```

The system should not falsely claim successful concept discovery.

---

# 6. Explicit / Encoded / Removed Conditions

Benchmark families should include:

### Explicit

The relevant information is directly available.

### Encoded

The relevant information is available only through a transformation requiring a more expressive model.

### Removed

The relevant information is absent from the current representation.

The purpose is to distinguish:

```text
information available but difficult to extract
```

from:

```text
information unavailable to the representation
```

---

# 7. Hidden Ground Truth

The benchmark may maintain hidden ground-truth state and concept annotations.

UNKNOWN must not receive:

* hidden state;
* hidden concept labels;
* hidden causal graph;
* hidden simulator variables;
* hidden transfer environment metadata.

Ground truth remains an evaluation artifact.

---

# 8. Frozen Benchmark Contract

Before the final experiment begins, freeze:

* observation interface;
* representation language;
* candidate grammar;
* model-family set;
* optimization budget;
* candidate search budget;
* complexity metric;
* adequacy thresholds;
* validation splits;
* intervention families;
* transfer families;
* stopping criteria.

Changes after observing final results must invalidate the affected comparison or be explicitly documented as a new experiment.

---

# 9. Representation Adequacy Score

The project should not immediately reduce all evidence to a single scalar.

Maintain separate measurements for:

$$
Q_{pred}
$$

predictive quality;

$$
Q_{int}
$$

interventional quality;

$$
Q_{fals}
$$

falsification performance;

$$
Q_{transfer}
$$

transfer performance;

$$
Q_{complexity}
$$

representation complexity/search cost.

A final aggregate score may be introduced later only if scientifically justified.

---

# 10. Prediction Evaluation

Prediction evaluation must use data not used for candidate construction.

Measurements may include:

* predictive error;
* calibration;
* likelihood;
* task-specific accuracy;
* uncertainty quality.

The exact metric must be selected according to the environment.

Prediction improvement alone cannot establish discovery.

---

# 11. Intervention Evaluation

Where the environment permits controlled interventions, candidate representations must generate predictions about intervention outcomes.

The protocol records:

* intervention;
* predicted outcome;
* observed outcome;
* prediction error;
* confidence;
* whether the intervention was used during candidate construction.

Interventions used for discovery must be separated from final intervention evaluation where feasible.

---

# 12. Falsification Evaluation

Every candidate must declare at least one predefined falsification condition.

The evaluation attempts to produce observations under which the candidate should fail if its hypothesis is incorrect.

A candidate that cannot be meaningfully falsified is not sufficient for a strong discovery claim.

---

# 13. Transfer Evaluation

Transfer environments must differ from discovery environments in controlled dimensions.

Possible variation dimensions include:

* object identities;
* surface attributes;
* initial conditions;
* parameter values;
* irrelevant visual properties;
* environment layout;
* temporal configuration.

The exact transfer distribution must be declared before final evaluation.

---

# 14. Representation Equivalence

The benchmark should not require exact symbolic recovery when multiple representations are scientifically equivalent.

Define:

$$
R_1\sim_{\mathcal{E}}R_2
$$

when they satisfy the predefined empirical equivalence criteria under evaluation suite \(\mathcal{E}\).

Equivalence must be tested across multiple dimensions rather than prediction alone.

---

# 15. Ground-Truth Equivalence Classes

Where possible, benchmark designers may construct known equivalence classes:

```text
Hidden structure
    ├── Representation A
    ├── Representation B
    └── Representation C
```

UNKNOWN is successful if it discovers an admissible member that satisfies the declared validation criteria.

Exact recovery can still be recorded as a separate diagnostic metric.

---

# 16. Decoy Candidates

Each benchmark family should include candidates that are deliberately attractive but scientifically invalid.

Examples:

* discovery-set shortcuts;
* correlations that fail under intervention;
* surface features that fail transfer;
* overly complex expressions;
* representations exploiting accidental simulator regularities.

A valid evaluation must measure rejection of such candidates.

---

# 17. Benchmark Leakage Controls

The benchmark designer must not:

* expose the hidden concept name;
* expose the target variable;
* construct the extension grammar specifically around the answer after observing results;
* provide transfer environments during discovery;
* allow test performance to guide candidate generation;
* modify thresholds after seeing results;
* choose only environments where the expected concept is easy to discover.

---

# 18. Search-Budget Controls

UNKNOWN and relevant baselines should receive declared budgets for:

* environment interactions;
* candidate evaluations;
* model training;
* representation search;
* computation;
* intervention count.

If exact computational equality is impossible, the difference must be measured and reported.

---

# 19. Baseline Suite

The initial baseline suite should include:

### Baseline A — Fixed representation

No representation extension.

### Baseline B — Parameter optimization

Same representation, stronger optimization.

### Baseline C — Model-family expansion

Same representation, broader permitted model families.

### Baseline D — Predictive feature search

Search for features that improve predictive performance.

### Baseline E — Latent representation search

Unsupervised or self-supervised representation learning where appropriate.

### Baseline F — Always-on representation search

Representation search without an explicit failure diagnosis trigger.

### Baseline G — UNKNOWN

Failure diagnosis followed by constrained representation extension and scientific validation.

---

# 20. Primary Ablation

The primary mechanism-level comparison is:

```text
UNKNOWN:
diagnose failure
    ↓
search representation
    ↓
validate
```

versus:

```text
Always-on search:
search representation
    ↓
validate
```

The comparison asks whether explicit failure diagnosis contributes measurable scientific or computational value.

---

# 21. Failure-Diagnosis Metric

UNKNOWN should produce a diagnosis before representation extension:

```text
PARAMETER_FAILURE
MODEL_FAMILY_FAILURE
REPRESENTATION_FAILURE
LANGUAGE_INSUFFICIENCY
UNRESOLVED
```

The hidden benchmark label is used only for evaluation.

Measure:

* diagnosis accuracy;
* confusion matrix;
* false representation-failure rate;
* missed representation-failure rate;
* unresolved rate.

No single diagnostic accuracy number should replace the underlying confusion matrix.

---

# 22. Discovery Metrics

Record:

* candidate count;
* valid candidate count;
* rejected candidate count;
* time/interactions to first valid candidate;
* search budget consumed;
* candidate complexity;
* validation performance;
* transfer performance;
* intervention performance;
* reproducibility across random seeds.

---

# 23. False Discovery Rate

A major metric should measure how frequently UNKNOWN declares a candidate valid when the candidate fails independent validation.

Conceptually:

$$
FDR_{candidate}
=
\frac{False\ Valid\ Candidates}
{All\ Validity\ Claims}
$$

The exact statistical estimator will be finalized during benchmark design.

---

# 24. Negative Results

Negative results are first-class outcomes.

Examples:

```text
No representation failure detected.
```

```text
Representation failure detected but no candidate found.
```

```text
Candidate found but rejected by intervention.
```

```text
Candidate passed discovery but failed transfer.
```

```text
Language insufficient.
```

These outcomes must remain visible in the experiment record.

---

# 25. Reproducibility

Every experiment must record:

* repository commit;
* configuration;
* random seeds;
* environment version;
* representation language version;
* model-family version;
* candidate-search budget;
* dataset/environment identifiers;
* generated candidate definitions;
* evaluation outputs.

A result that cannot be reconstructed from recorded artifacts is incomplete evidence.

---

# 26. Acceptance Criterion

A candidate may be classified as a validated representation discovery only when:

$$
Diagnosis
\land
Prediction
\land
Intervention
\land
Falsification
\land
Transfer
\land
ConstraintCompliance
$$

are satisfied according to the predefined protocol.

Not every environment must support every test dimension.

When a dimension is unavailable, the limitation must be explicitly recorded rather than silently removed.

---

# 27. Research-Level Success

Project UNKNOWN does not require that UNKNOWN outperform every baseline on every metric.

The central scientific question is narrower:

> Does explicit representation-failure diagnosis provide measurable and reproducible value beyond parameter optimization, model-family selection, predictive feature search, latent representation search, and always-on representation search?

Possible outcomes include:

1. evidence supporting the hypothesis;
2. evidence against the hypothesis;
3. evidence that the effect occurs only under certain environment classes;
4. evidence that another mechanism explains the observed improvement;
5. inability to distinguish the mechanism with the proposed benchmark.

All outcomes are scientifically meaningful.

---

# 28. Kill Conditions

The research direction should be reconsidered if:

1. UNKNOWN cannot reliably distinguish representation failure from model-family failure;
2. always-on representation search consistently reproduces the same results without diagnosis;
3. improvements disappear under independent transfer;
4. candidates repeatedly fail intervention testing;
5. candidate validity depends primarily on benchmark leakage;
6. representation language constraints dominate the result;
7. stronger conventional baselines explain the observed gains;
8. the proposed mechanism cannot be operationalized reproducibly.

---

# 29. Current Status

This is a working evaluation specification.

It must be reviewed against the final synthetic environment design before implementation.

No benchmark result may be interpreted as evidence for the research hypothesis until the evaluation protocol is frozen and the relevant baselines are implemented.
