# S0.5-C — Public Observation Projection Contract

**Project:** Project UNKNOWN
**Sprint:** S0.5-C
**Stage:** Public Observation Projection
**Status:** Contract
**Date:** 2026-09-23

---

## 1. Purpose

S0.5-C defines the scientific and engineering contract for projecting internal synthetic-world state into the public observation interface available to UNKNOWN.

The projection is represented as:

$$
O_t=P(S_t)
$$

where:

* \(S_t\) is the internal world state at time \(t\);
* \(P\) is the public observation projection;
* \(O_t\) is the observation legitimately available to UNKNOWN.

The purpose of this contract is to ensure that the public observation channel is explicit, deterministic, reproducible, auditable, and isolated from hidden benchmark truth and evaluation state.

---

## 2. Scientific Role

The observation projection is a scientific boundary, not merely a serialization layer.

The information presented to UNKNOWN determines what can legitimately be learned from the environment.

Therefore:

$$
\text{Internal State} \neq \text{Public Observation}
$$

and:

$$
\text{Public Observation} \neq \text{Evaluation Truth}
$$

The projection must not expose information merely because it exists inside the simulator.

---

## 3. Relationship to the Research Question

Project UNKNOWN investigates whether a system can detect and respond to systematic inadequacy in its current representation.

The relevant distinction is:

$$
\text{Internal State}
\rightarrow
\text{Public Information}
\rightarrow
\text{Current Representation}
\rightarrow
\text{Candidate Extension}
$$

The public observation boundary therefore determines the information available to the discovery system.

A variable may be:

1. directly observable;
2. derivable from permitted observation history;
3. present internally but not recoverable from public observations;
4. represented poorly by the current representation despite being inferable from observations.

These cases must not be conflated.

---

## 4. Formal Environment Interface

The synthetic environment is represented as:

$$
E=(S,A,O,T,I)
$$

where:

* \(S\) is internal state;
* \(A\) is the action space;
* \(O\) is the public observation space;
* \(T\) is the transition function;
* \(I\) is the intervention interface.

The observation function is:

$$
P:S\rightarrow O
$$

For a trajectory:

$$
O_{\leq t}=(O_0,O_1,\ldots,O_t)
$$

UNKNOWN may construct its representation from the permitted observation history:

$$
R_t=R(O_{\leq t})
$$

---

## 5. Information Classes

All environment information is classified into three primary scientific boundaries.

### 5.1 Public Information

Information intentionally exposed through the public environment interface.

Examples include:

* public entity identity;
* entity position;
* entity velocity;
* explicitly observable relations;
* public events;
* simulation step index where permitted;
* deterministic simulation timestamp.

Public information may be used by UNKNOWN.

### 5.2 Hidden Information

Information maintained internally by the simulator or benchmark but not exposed through the public observation interface.

Examples include:

* internal mass;
* semantic entity category;
* hidden benchmark condition;
* ground-truth representation;
* hidden causal structure;
* evaluation outcomes;
* transfer labels;
* falsification labels;
* benchmark construction metadata.

Hidden information must not enter the public projection.

### 5.3 Evaluation Information

Information used to determine whether UNKNOWN succeeded.

Examples include:

* ground-truth variables;
* benchmark condition labels;
* expected representation;
* evaluation scores;
* intervention ground truth;
* transfer ground truth;
* falsification outcomes.

Evaluation information must remain outside the public environment projection.

---

## 6. Public Primitive Observation Contract

The core public observation consists of primitive information deliberately exposed by the environment.

The projection may expose:

```text
observation_id
step_index
kind
entities
relations
events
timestamp
```

The contents of each field are governed by the rules below.

---

## 7. Public Entity Projection

Each internal entity may be projected into:

```text
PublicEntity
```

The public entity representation may contain:

```text
entity_id
position
velocity
attributes
```

### 7.1 Entity Identifier

The public identifier must be:

* deterministic;
* unique within the required scope;
* semantically opaque;
* independent of hidden benchmark conditions;
* independent of evaluation labels.

Identifiers must not encode semantic information.

Invalid examples include:

```text
heavy-object
target-agent
massive_entity
hidden-category-1
representation-failure-entity
```

Acceptable identifiers follow an opaque deterministic convention such as:

```text
entity-000
entity-001
entity-002
```

The identifier is an identity mechanism, not a semantic feature.

---

## 8. Position

Position is a public primitive observation.

For entity \(i\):

$$
p_i=(x_i,y_i)
$$

The public projection exposes:

```text
position.x
position.y
```

subject to the deterministic world bounds.

Position must be copied from the current internal state and must not be modified by the projection.

---

## 9. Velocity

Velocity is a public primitive observation under the established S0.5 environment contract.

For entity \(i\):

$$
v_i=(v_{x_i},v_{y_i})
$$

The public projection exposes:

```text
velocity.x
velocity.y
```

Velocity is intentionally public because it is already part of the established environment observation contract.

The benchmark must therefore not treat direct recovery of velocity as sufficient evidence of autonomous concept discovery.

---

## 10. Internal Mass

Mass remains internal.

The projection must not directly expose:

```text
mass
```

or an equivalent semantic encoding of mass.

Mass may influence observable dynamics, allowing controlled experiments in which its effects are available through observations without revealing the simulator's internal mass value.

This distinction supports separation between:

$$
\text{observable consequence}
$$

and:

$$
\text{hidden simulator variable}
$$

---

## 11. Internal Entity Category

Internal semantic categories such as:

```text
AGENT
OBJECT
LANDMARK
```

must not be automatically exposed.

These categories represent simulator-side semantics and could constitute predefined abstractions if injected directly into UNKNOWN.

A future benchmark may intentionally make a category observable, but such a condition must be explicitly defined as part of the public information contract rather than leaking through implementation.

---

## 12. Public Attributes

`PublicEntity.attributes` may contain only explicitly declared public attributes.

The projection must not copy arbitrary internal entity properties into the attributes mapping.

In particular, it must not automatically serialize:

* mass;
* internal category;
* hidden variables;
* benchmark condition;
* ground-truth representation;
* evaluation labels;
* causal annotations;
* oracle outcomes.

The allowed public attribute set must be controlled by configuration.

---

## 13. Derived Quantities

Derived quantities are not automatically public primitives.

The projection must not silently add features such as:

```text
distance
relative_position
relative_velocity
acceleration
speed
nearest_entity
collision_probability
interaction_strength
trajectory_class
```

unless a future contract explicitly declares the quantity observable.

This preserves a distinction between:

$$
\text{raw public information}
$$

and:

$$
\text{features constructed by UNKNOWN}
$$

---

## 14. Temporal History

UNKNOWN may use permitted observation history:

$$
O_{\leq t}=(O_0,\ldots,O_t)
$$

The environment does not label derived quantities as concepts merely because they can be computed from history.

For example, given successive public positions:

$$
p_{t-1},p_t
$$

UNKNOWN may derive a displacement:

$$
\Delta p_t=p_t-p_{t-1}
$$

Such a quantity is derivable information, not a simulator-provided semantic label.

The observation projection must therefore remain a primitive-information boundary.

---

## 15. Relations

Relations are public only when explicitly part of the public environment configuration.

A public relation contains:

```text
relation_id
source_entity_id
target_entity_id
relation_type
value
```

Relation identifiers must be opaque and deterministic.

Source and target identifiers must refer only to public entity identifiers.

The projection must not inject hidden causal semantics into relation fields.

---

## 16. Relation Type

`relation_type` is itself observable information.

Therefore its semantics must be intentionally defined by the benchmark.

The projection must not encode hidden benchmark conditions through relation names.

Invalid examples include:

```text
representation_failure_relation
hidden_mass_relation
target_condition_relation
ground_truth_relation
```

If relation types represent genuine public environmental structure, they must be documented as such.

---

## 17. Events

Events are public only when they are explicitly part of the observation contract.

Events must:

* originate from public environmental behavior;
* contain no hidden benchmark labels;
* contain no evaluation outcomes;
* remain deterministic under deterministic replay;
* not encode hidden state indirectly.

An event string must not become a covert channel for benchmark truth.

---

## 18. Observation Kind

The observation kind describes the public observation class.

The current contract permits:

```text
STATE
EVENT
```

The value must describe the public observation itself and must not encode hidden experiment conditions.

---

## 19. Step Index

The step index is public operational information.

For observation \(O_t\):

$$
step\_index=t
$$

The value must be deterministic and consistent with the world transition.

It must not vary according to hidden benchmark conditions.

---

## 20. Timestamp

The timestamp represents deterministic simulation time.

It must not use wall-clock time.

For a fixed deterministic configuration:

$$
timestamp_t=f(t,\Delta t)
$$

and must be reproducible across equivalent runs.

The current minimal runtime may use the deterministic simulation step as its timestamp representation.

---

## 21. Observation Identifier

Observation identifiers must be:

* deterministic;
* unique for the relevant episode scope;
* semantically opaque;
* independent of hidden truth;
* independent of evaluation outcomes.

They must not encode:

* benchmark condition;
* hidden variable;
* candidate concept;
* evaluation result;
* transfer result.

---

## 22. Ordering

Public entity and relation ordering must be deterministic.

Ordering must not depend on:

* hidden condition labels;
* hidden variable values;
* evaluation results;
* candidate quality;
* benchmark split;
* discovery outcome.

Where multiple valid internal orderings exist, the projection must use an explicit deterministic ordering rule.

---

## 23. Projection Purity

The projection should behave as a pure transformation with respect to the public result:

$$
P(S_t)=O_t
$$

It must not:

* mutate the internal world state;
* modify entity state;
* modify relation state;
* modify hidden benchmark state;
* modify evaluation state;
* perform network access;
* consult external services;
* generate nondeterministic semantic values.

---

## 24. Projection Determinism

For equivalent internal states and equivalent public configuration:

$$
S_t=S'_t
$$

must imply:

$$
P(S_t)=P(S'_t)
$$

apart from explicitly documented object-identity details that do not affect serialized public content.

Repeated projection of an unchanged state must produce equivalent public observations.

---

## 25. Projection Isolation

The projection implementation must import only the modules required to transform internal dynamics into public schemas.

It must not depend on:

```text
hidden/
evaluation/
```

for normal public projection.

The public observation layer must not gain access to benchmark truth merely for convenience.

---

## 26. Hidden-State Isolation

The following must never cross into the public observation:

```text
HiddenState
HiddenVariable
GroundTruthRepresentation
HiddenCondition
EvaluationRecord
DiscoveryEvaluation
```

No direct or indirect serialization of these structures is permitted.

---

## 27. Configuration Boundary

Public configuration may explicitly determine:

* world dimensions;
* entity count;
* observation history limit;
* allowed actions;
* allowed interventions;
* public attribute names.

The projection must respect this configuration.

Configuration itself must not expose hidden benchmark condition labels.

---

## 28. Information Availability

A hidden variable \(X\) may be considered information-available only when its relevant distinction can be inferred from permitted public observations and interaction history under the benchmark's assumptions.

Conceptually:

$$
X\in\mathcal I(O_{\leq t},A_{\leq t},I_{\leq t})
$$

This does not imply that \(X\) is directly observable.

---

## 29. Information Unavailable

A variable belongs to an information-unavailable condition when the relevant distinction cannot be recovered from the permitted public information and interaction interface.

Conceptually:

$$
X\notin\mathcal I(O_{\leq t},A_{\leq t},I_{\leq t})
$$

The benchmark must distinguish this condition from failure of the current representation.

---

## 30. Representation Failure

A representation failure occurs when relevant information is available through permitted observations or interactions but is absent from the current representation.

Conceptually:

$$
X\in\mathcal I(O_{\leq t},A_{\leq t},I_{\leq t})
$$

while:

$$
X\notin R(O_{\leq t},A_{\leq t},I_{\leq t})
$$

The observation projection must provide sufficient information for such conditions to be scientifically possible without directly revealing the target representation.

---

## 31. Model-Family Failure

A model-family failure occurs when the relevant information is already represented but the selected model family cannot express the required dependency.

Conceptually:

$$
X\in R
$$

but:

$$
\forall M\in\mathcal M(R):
Score(M,R)<\tau
$$

while an allowed stronger model family can satisfy the criterion.

The observation projection must not be designed in a way that confounds this condition with missing information.

---

## 32. Parameter Failure

Parameter failure occurs when the representation and model family are adequate but the selected parameterization or optimization state is inadequate.

The observation channel must remain unchanged across paired parameter-failure conditions.

---

## 33. Paired Counterfactual Principle

The observation channel must remain controlled across paired benchmark conditions wherever the scientific comparison requires it.

For example, a model-family comparison should not secretly change the public observation channel.

Likewise, a representation-failure comparison should not be produced merely by changing public information unless the benchmark explicitly defines that as the information condition.

The benchmark must make the causal source of failure identifiable.

---

## 34. No Oracle Features

The projection must never provide features whose semantics directly identify the target benchmark concept solely to make discovery easier.

For example, if the hidden target is a persistent physical property, exposing:

```text
property_class
property_value
ground_truth_type
```

would invalidate the intended discovery experiment.

---

## 35. Leakage Threat Model

The projection must be audited against at least the following leakage classes:

### Identifier leakage

* semantic identifiers;
* condition-coded identifiers;
* ordered IDs reflecting hidden labels.

### Attribute leakage

* hidden values copied into public attributes;
* semantic category leakage;
* ground-truth fields.

### Relation leakage

* hidden condition encoded in relation types;
* hidden values encoded in relation values.

### Ordering leakage

* hidden-condition-dependent ordering;
* evaluation-dependent ordering.

### Metadata leakage

* benchmark labels;
* condition names;
* evaluation metadata;
* hidden split information.

### Timing leakage

* wall-clock behavior;
* condition-dependent timing;
* condition-dependent observation frequency.

### Serialization leakage

* hidden fields included in serialized objects;
* debug representations;
* exception messages.

### Import/object leakage

* public projection importing hidden or evaluation modules;
* object references exposing internal state.

---

## 36. Error Contract

Errors originating from public projection must not reveal hidden benchmark information.

Error messages must not contain:

* hidden condition names;
* ground-truth variables;
* evaluation results;
* hidden state values;
* benchmark construction details.

Errors should expose only information necessary to diagnose legitimate public API misuse or implementation failure.

---

## 37. External Dependency Constraint

The public observation projection must not depend on external:

* network services;
* model APIs;
* LLM providers;
* databases outside the experiment boundary;
* nondeterministic external processes.

The projection must be locally reproducible.

---

## 38. Reproducibility

Given:

$$
(config,seed,A_{1:n},I_{1:m})
$$

equivalent executions must produce equivalent public observation trajectories:

$$
O_{0:n}
$$

and equivalent public serialized content.

This requirement is necessary for controlled comparison and scientific replication.

---

## 39. Public Serialization Contract

When public observations are serialized, serialization must preserve only public schema information.

Serialization must not expose:

* internal object references;
* hidden state;
* evaluation objects;
* private attributes;
* Python implementation details that reveal hidden semantics.

Serialization output must remain deterministic.

---

## 40. Projection Test Requirements

The implementation must include automated tests for:

### Correctness

* valid internal state produces valid public observation;
* positions are preserved;
* velocities are preserved;
* permitted attributes are exposed;
* forbidden attributes are absent;
* relations are projected correctly;
* events are projected correctly.

### Determinism

* repeated projection is stable;
* equivalent states produce equivalent observations;
* equivalent runs produce equivalent trajectories.

### Isolation

* hidden state is not exposed;
* evaluation state is not exposed;
* public objects do not reference hidden objects.

### Ordering

* entity ordering is deterministic;
* relation ordering is deterministic;
* ordering is independent of hidden semantics.

### Configuration

* public attribute configuration is respected;
* disallowed public fields are not emitted.

### Leakage

* semantic identifiers are rejected;
* hidden fields cannot enter public attributes;
* hidden condition labels cannot appear in serialized observations;
* error messages do not expose hidden truth.

---

## 41. Scientific Acceptance Criteria

S0.5-C is scientifically acceptable only if:

1. the public observation contract is explicit;
2. every public field has a documented scientific justification;
3. internal-only fields are explicitly excluded;
4. derived features are not silently injected;
5. hidden and evaluation schemas remain isolated;
6. deterministic replay is preserved;
7. observation ordering is controlled;
8. information-unavailable and representation-failure conditions remain distinguishable in principle;
9. the observation channel does not encode benchmark labels;
10. automated leakage tests cover the defined threat model.

## 46. Known Limitations

The current contract does not claim that the chosen public channel is sufficient for every future benchmark condition.

Future benchmark construction may require:

* additional observable relations;
* richer events;
* controlled partial observability;
* temporal windows;
* action-conditioned observations;
* intervention-specific observation semantics.

Such changes require explicit contract updates and corresponding scientific review.

The observation channel must not be modified opportunistically to make a later experiment succeed.

---

## 47. Design Principle

The public observation layer must satisfy:

$$
\boxed{
\text{Enough information to make scientific discovery possible}
}
$$

without becoming:

$$
\boxed{
\text{an oracle for the intended discovery}
}
$$

Therefore:

$$
\text{Public Observation}
\rightarrow
\text{Information Available}
\rightarrow
\text{Representation}
$$

must remain experimentally distinguishable.

---

## 48. Contract Principle

The observation projection is a scientific instrument.

It defines what UNKNOWN is allowed to know.

Therefore:

> **The simulator may know more than UNKNOWN. The evaluator may know more than UNKNOWN. Neither may silently communicate that additional knowledge through the public observation channel.**

The public projection must expose legitimate environmental evidence while preserving the possibility of information failure, representation failure, model-family failure, and parameter failure as distinct experimental conditions.

---

with hidden benchmark truth and evaluation state remaining outside this path.
