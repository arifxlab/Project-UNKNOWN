# 1. Purpose

This document is an adversarial analysis of the proposed research problem for Project UNKNOWN.

Its purpose is not to establish novelty.

Its purpose is to determine whether the proposed problem remains scientifically meaningful and sufficiently distinguishable from existing work before implementation begins.

The analysis deliberately assumes that the proposed idea may already exist in substantially equivalent form.

If prior work demonstrates essentially the same complete mechanism, the research direction must be narrowed, reformulated, or rejected.

---

# 2. Candidate Research Problem

The current candidate problem is:

> Can an artificial system explicitly detect when its current representation is inadequate, distinguish representation-level inadequacy from parameter and model-family failure, autonomously construct a candidate representation extension, and experimentally validate that extension through prediction, intervention, falsification, and transfer to unseen contexts?

This is a provisional research question.

It is not a novelty claim.

---

# 3. Candidate Research Boundary

The current proposed boundary is:

```text
Systematic model failure
        ↓
Failure diagnosis
        ↓
Parameter failure?
        ↓ no
Model-family failure?
        ↓ no
Representation-level failure?
        ↓ yes
Representation extension
        ↓
Candidate hypothesis
        ↓
Prediction
        ↓
Intervention
        ↓
Falsification
        ↓
Unseen-context transfer
```

The central candidate distinction is therefore not:

> "Can an AI discover concepts?"

It is:

> "Can an artificial system determine that its current representational vocabulary is the source of a persistent failure, rather than merely its parameters or model family, and use that diagnosis to trigger a scientifically testable representation extension?"

---

# 4. Prior-Art Adversarial Findings

The initial literature investigation demonstrates substantial overlap with existing research.

## 4.1 Concept and Representation Discovery

Concept discovery and representation learning are established research areas.

Therefore:

> Concept discovery alone cannot constitute the research contribution.

A system that simply discovers useful latent variables, features, clusters, or concepts is insufficient.

---

## 4.2 Causal Representation Learning

Causal representation learning explicitly investigates recovering high-level latent factors from lower-level observations.

Interventional causal representation learning further studies how intervention data can facilitate identification of latent causal factors.

Therefore:

> "Use interventions to discover or validate representations" is not itself a sufficient distinction.

The relevant distinction would need to concern the **diagnosis-triggered transition from an inadequate representation to a new representation**, rather than representation learning with interventions in general.

---

## 4.3 Causal Abstraction

Causal abstraction studies relationships between different representational levels and uses intervention-based criteria to evaluate whether higher-level representations faithfully capture lower-level behavior.

Therefore:

> Multi-level representation and intervention-based abstraction validation are already established concepts.

UNKNOWN cannot claim novelty merely from having multiple representational levels or intervention-based validation.

---

## 4.4 Theory Revision

Theory revision already studies systems whose existing theories are incomplete, imperfect, or incorrect and need to be revised.

Therefore:

> "The system detects failure and revises its theory" is not sufficient.

The research boundary must specify what makes the revision a **representation-level diagnosis** rather than ordinary theory or parameter revision.

---

## 4.5 Predicate / Concept Invention

Predicate invention and neural concept invention already provide mechanisms for introducing new representational units into symbolic or neurosymbolic systems.

Recent neurosymbolic theory-revision work explicitly combines theory revision with learned neural concepts and evaluates revisions against existing theories and baselines.

Therefore:

> "The system invents a new concept when the existing theory is inadequate" overlaps substantially with prior work.

UNKNOWN must not use concept invention itself as its novelty argument.

---

## 4.6 Missing Causal Variables

Work on hypothesizing missing causal variables explicitly studies the generation of hypotheses for variables absent from a partial causal graph.

Therefore:

> "The system hypothesizes a missing variable" is also insufficient as a standalone research contribution.

The candidate distinction would need to involve explicit diagnosis of the inadequacy of the current representation and controlled validation of the representation extension.

---

## 4.7 Automated Scientific Discovery

Automated scientific discovery systems already combine components such as:

* hypothesis generation;
* experiment design;
* experiment execution;
* analysis;
* iterative refinement.

Therefore:

> "UNKNOWN autonomously forms hypotheses and runs experiments" is not sufficient.

The scientific contribution, if any, must reside in the specific representation-failure problem and its operational evaluation.

# 6. Fundamental Failure Taxonomy

UNKNOWN requires three distinct failure levels.

## 6.1 Parameter Failure

The current representation contains sufficient information.

The model family is capable of expressing the target relationship.

The failure is caused by inadequate parameter values or estimation.

Formally, there exists an adequate model within the current representation and model family, but the currently estimated parameters do not achieve the required criterion.

```text
Representation: adequate
Model family: adequate
Parameters: inadequate
```

---

## 6.2 Model-Family Failure

The current representation contains the necessary information, but the selected model family cannot express the relevant relationship.

```text
Representation: adequate
Model family: inadequate
Parameters: potentially irrelevant
```

The expected remedy is a model-family change.

---

## 6.3 Representation Failure

The current representation lacks a distinction required to express the relevant reusable structure.

```text
Representation: inadequate
Model family: insufficiently relevant
Parameters: insufficiently relevant
```

The expected remedy is representation extension.

---

# 7. Why the Distinction Is Difficult

Observed predictive failure alone cannot identify representation failure.

Suppose:

```text
Prediction error is high.
```

This observation is compatible with:

* insufficient training;
* poor parameters;
* insufficient model capacity;
* incorrect inductive bias;
* distribution shift;
* noisy observations;
* incorrect target assumptions;
* missing variables;
* inadequate representation.

Therefore:

$$
\text{Prediction failure}
\not\Rightarrow
\text{Representation failure}
$$

The system needs controlled alternatives that test whether the relevant information is already available within the current representation.

---

# 8. Working Definition of Representation Adequacy

For a target variable \(Y\), representation \(R\), model family \(\mathcal{M}\), context set \(C\), and evaluation protocol \(E\), define a representation as provisionally adequate when there exists a model:

$$
M \in \mathcal{M}(R)
$$

such that:

$$
\operatorname{Score}(M,R;E,C) \geq \tau
$$

for a predefined threshold \(\tau\).

This is only a working definition.

It must be refined because a sufficiently powerful model family may conceal representational problems.

---

# 9. Working Definition of Representation Failure

A candidate representation \(R\) is provisionally considered inadequate for a task when:

$$
\forall M \in \mathcal{M}(R):
\operatorname{Score}(M,R;E,C) < \tau
$$

while an admissible representation extension \(R'\) permits:

$$
\exists M' \in \mathcal{M}(R'):
\operatorname{Score}(M',R';E,C) \geq \tau
$$

However, this condition alone is insufficient.

It could reward unrestricted feature engineering.

Therefore representation failure requires additional controls.

---

# 10. Representation Extension Constraints

A candidate representation extension must satisfy predefined constraints.

At minimum:

1. It must use only information available to UNKNOWN.
2. It must not access hidden ground truth.
3. Its construction procedure must be reproducible.
4. Its complexity must be measurable.
5. Its information source must be recorded.
6. Its construction must be separated from final validation.
7. Its predictions must be evaluated prospectively.
8. Its transfer behavior must be tested.
9. Its intervention behavior must be evaluated where applicable.
10. It must have predefined rejection conditions.

The representation search space must be bounded enough that discovery does not become unrestricted model search.

---

# 11. Discovery, Validation, and Test Separation

UNKNOWN must separate:

```text
Discovery Data
      ↓
Candidate Construction
      ↓
Validation Data
      ↓
Candidate Selection
      ↓
Locked Test / Transfer Data
      ↓
Final Evaluation
```

The final evaluation data must not be used to construct or iteratively tune the candidate.

Intervention outcomes used for candidate construction must be distinguished from interventions used for final evaluation.

Unseen-context evaluation must remain inaccessible during candidate optimization.

---

# 12. Candidate Representation

A candidate representation \(R'\) is a proposed extension to the current representation \(R\).

The extension may take forms such as:

* derived feature;
* predicate;
* latent variable;
* symbolic abstraction;
* structured variable;
* temporal abstraction;
* relational representation;
* compositional representation.

The allowed representation language must be defined before experiments.

A candidate is not accepted merely because it improves a metric.

---

# 13. Candidate Concept

For UNKNOWN, a candidate concept is a representational unit with:

1. explicit definition;
2. construction procedure;
3. measurable complexity;
4. expected behavioral consequences;
5. testable predictions;
6. predefined falsification conditions;
7. transfer criteria.

A human-readable name is not part of the scientific definition.

---

# 14. Prediction Requirement

Before observing validation outcomes, a candidate should generate predictions that can be compared with observations.

This prevents:

```text
Observation
    ↓
Post-hoc explanation
```

from being incorrectly treated as predictive evidence.

A stronger scientific sequence is:

```text
Candidate
    ↓
Prediction
    ↓
Observation
    ↓
Comparison
```

---

# 15. Intervention Requirement

Where the environment permits intervention, candidate representations should generate predictions about controlled changes.

An intervention should change a specified part of the environment while preserving as much unrelated structure as possible.

The candidate should predict the expected effect before observing the outcome.

The intervention protocol must specify:

* intervention space;
* intervention budget;
* selection policy;
* expected outcomes;
* competing hypotheses;
* rejection conditions.

Interventions are not automatically evidence for UNKNOWN's distinction because intervention-based representation learning already exists.

Their role here is to help determine whether the candidate representation explains reusable structure rather than merely correlating with observed outcomes.

---

# 16. Falsification Requirement

Every candidate must have conditions under which it can be rejected.

The system must be allowed to conclude:

```text
Candidate rejected.
```

A successful research system must therefore be capable of producing negative discoveries.

The evaluation framework must prevent the system from continuously modifying a candidate until it passes.

---

# 17. Transfer Requirement

A representation discovered in environment or context \(C_1\) must be evaluated in contexts that were not available during discovery.

Conceptually:

$$
R'_{C_1}
\rightarrow
C_2,C_3,\ldots
$$

A candidate that succeeds only in its discovery context may represent:

* memorization;
* context-specific correlation;
* accidental feature engineering;
* dataset-specific structure.

Transfer therefore provides evidence about reusability.

Transfer success does not by itself prove that a representation is scientifically meaningful.

---

# 18. False Candidate Requirement

The environment must contain patterns that can produce plausible but incorrect candidate representations.

Examples may include:

* spurious correlations;
* context-specific variables;
* coincidental clusters;
* proxy variables;
* short-term predictive features;
* variables that fail under intervention.

This is necessary because a system that never encounters false candidates cannot demonstrate meaningful falsification.

---

# 19. Alternative Explanation Problem

Any apparent success by UNKNOWN must be tested against alternative explanations.

Possible alternatives include:

1. increased model capacity;
2. larger search space;
3. additional training;
4. accidental feature engineering;
5. data leakage;
6. memorization;
7. context-specific correlations;
8. intervention leakage;
9. test-set contamination;
10. arbitrary representation expansion.

Therefore every major experiment requires controlled baselines and ablations.

---

# 20. Required Baselines

The minimum baseline family should include:

### Baseline A — Fixed Representation

No representation extension.

---

### Baseline B — Parameter Optimization

Current representation retained while allowing extensive parameter optimization.

---

### Baseline C — Model-Family Expansion

Current representation retained while allowing stronger or alternative model families.

---

### Baseline D — Statistical Feature Discovery

Feature discovery without explicit representation-failure diagnosis.

---

### Baseline E — Latent Representation Discovery

Latent-variable or representation learning without the UNKNOWN failure-trigger mechanism.

---

### Baseline F — Always-On Representation Search

Representation discovery occurs without first diagnosing representation-level failure.

This baseline is especially important.

If UNKNOWN does not outperform or provide scientific advantages over always-on representation search, the failure-trigger mechanism may not be doing meaningful work.

---

# 21. Core Ablation

The most important planned ablation is:

```text
UNKNOWN:
Failure diagnosis
      ↓
Representation extension
      ↓
Validation

versus

Ablation:
Representation search
      ↓
Validation
```

If both systems behave equivalently, the explicit diagnosis mechanism may not justify its additional complexity.

---

# 22. Stronger Ablation

A second important comparison is:

```text
Current representation
      ↓
Stronger model family
      ↓
Validation
```

versus:

```text
Current representation
      ↓
Diagnosis
      ↓
Representation extension
      ↓
Validation
```

This tests whether representation extension is actually necessary.

---

# 23. Potential Scientific Contribution

The current candidate contribution can therefore be stated as:

> An experimentally testable framework for determining whether persistent predictive/modeling failure reflects a deficiency in the current representation itself, and for evaluating whether a diagnosis-triggered representation extension produces reusable explanatory or predictive structure under intervention, falsification, and unseen-context transfer.

This remains a candidate contribution.

It is not yet a novelty claim.

---

# 24. Strongest Objections

## Objection 1

This is merely model selection.

### Response

If UNKNOWN cannot distinguish representation failure from model-family failure using controlled model-family alternatives, the objection succeeds.

This is therefore a required falsification test.

---

## Objection 2

This is merely feature engineering.

### Response

If representation extensions can be reduced to ordinary feature search without meaningful differences in diagnosis, validation, or transfer, the proposed distinction weakens substantially.

---

## Objection 3

This is theory revision under another name.

### Response

Existing theory-revision systems already revise imperfect theories and may introduce learned concepts.

UNKNOWN must therefore demonstrate whether its explicit representation-failure diagnosis and experimental validation protocol constitute a meaningfully different problem.

If not, the scope must be narrowed or the project rejected.

---

## Objection 4

The system is simply searching a larger hypothesis space.

### Response

Representation extension must be complexity-controlled and compared against equally capable search baselines.

---

## Objection 5

Transfer does not prove conceptual validity.

### Response

Correct.

Transfer is evidence for reuse, not proof of conceptual truth.

It must therefore be combined with prediction, intervention, and falsification.

---

## Objection 6

Interventions already exist in causal representation learning.

### Response

Correct.

Intervention is not claimed as novel.

Its proposed role is specifically to distinguish candidate representations from observational shortcuts after a representation-failure diagnosis.

---

## Objection 7

A sufficiently powerful model can learn around representation limitations.

### Response

This is a central experimental problem.

The representation language and model family must therefore be explicitly specified.

Otherwise the representation-failure claim is undefined.

---

# 25. Required Scientific Test

The candidate research direction should survive the following sequence.

### Test 1 — Diagnose

Can UNKNOWN identify systematic failure?

### Test 2 — Exclude Parameter Failure

Can sufficiently strong parameter optimization be shown inadequate?

### Test 3 — Exclude Model-Family Failure

Can sufficiently strong alternative model families be shown inadequate under the current representation?

### Test 4 — Construct

Can UNKNOWN generate a constrained representation extension?

### Test 5 — Predict

Can the candidate make prospective predictions?

### Test 6 — Intervene

Can the candidate predict controlled intervention outcomes?

### Test 7 — Falsify

Can deliberately misleading candidates be rejected?

### Test 8 — Transfer

Does the candidate remain useful in unseen contexts?

### Test 9 — Compare

Does the complete system outperform or scientifically distinguish itself from strong baselines and ablations?

### Test 10 — Reproduce

Do independent runs reproduce the effect?

Failure at any critical stage weakens the research claim.

# 26. Candidate Mathematical Formulation

Let:

$$
E = (S,A,O,T)
$$

where:

* \(S\) is hidden state;
* \(A\) is the intervention/action space;
* \(O\) is the observation process;
* \(T\) is the environment transition or data-generating mechanism.

The system observes:

$$
o_t \sim O(s_t)
$$

and constructs:

$$
r_t = R(o_{\leq t})
$$

A predictive model operates over:

$$
M(R)
$$

Let:

$$
\mathcal{M}(R)
$$

denote the permitted model family operating on representation \(R\).

A provisional adequacy condition is:

$$
\exists M \in \mathcal{M}(R)
:
\operatorname{Score}(M,R;E,C)
\geq \tau
$$

where \(C\) denotes the relevant contexts and \(\tau\) is a predefined criterion.

A provisional representation-failure condition is:

$$
\forall M \in \mathcal{M}(R):
\operatorname{Score}(M,R;E,C)
< \tau
$$

while an admissible extension \(R'\) satisfies:

$$
\exists M' \in \mathcal{M}(R'):
\operatorname{Score}(M',R';E,C)
\geq \tau
$$

with additional requirements:

$$
R'
\rightarrow
\text{prospective prediction}
\rightarrow
\text{intervention}
\rightarrow
\text{falsification}
\rightarrow
\text{transfer}
$$

This formulation is intentionally incomplete.

S0.3 must determine whether these definitions can be made operational without circularity.

# 27. Critical Unresolved Issue: The Counterfactual Representation Test

The deepest unresolved question is:

> How can the system determine that the current representation is inadequate without already knowing the representation that should exist?

A naive approach is:

```text
Current model fails
    ↓
Try new features
    ↓
Feature improves model
    ↓
Declare representation failure
```

This is insufficient.

The improvement could result from:

* additional capacity;
* overfitting;
* accidental correlations;
* leakage;
* feature engineering;
* model selection.

Therefore UNKNOWN requires a stronger diagnostic criterion.

Possible future approaches include:

* controlled expressivity tests;
* equivalence classes of model families;
* information-theoretic tests;
* invariance violations;
* residual structure tests;
* context-dependent failure patterns;
* intervention-conditioned residuals;
* minimum-description-length comparisons;
* causal sufficiency tests.

None is selected yet.

---

# 28. Critical Unresolved Issue: Representation Language

UNKNOWN cannot claim representation failure without defining what counts as a permissible representation.

Potential representation languages include:

* scalar variables;
* categorical variables;
* symbolic predicates;
* graphs;
* programs;
* temporal abstractions;
* compositional objects;
* learned latent variables.

The first benchmark should use a deliberately constrained representation language.

Otherwise the search space becomes too unrestricted for scientific interpretation.

---

# 29. Critical Unresolved Issue: Ground Truth

The first environment should contain a known hidden mechanism.

However:

```text
Ground Truth
     ↓
Experiment Designer
```

must be separated from:

```text
Observations
     ↓
UNKNOWN
```

UNKNOWN must never receive the hidden mechanism directly.

Ground truth is used only for:

* experiment construction;
* post-hoc scientific evaluation;
* failure analysis.

---

# 30. Critical Unresolved Issue: Benchmark Leakage

The benchmark must not encode the expected answer in a way that makes discovery trivial.

For example, if a variable is named:

```text
hidden_concept
```

or if candidate generation has access to ground-truth metadata, the experiment becomes invalid.

All hidden information must remain outside the system's accessible state.

---

# 31. Kill Criteria for the Research Direction

The candidate research direction should be reconsidered or terminated if:

1. an existing system is found that implements essentially the same complete mechanism;
2. representation failure cannot be operationally distinguished from model-family failure;
3. the representation language cannot be bounded coherently;
4. representation extension reduces to unrestricted feature search;
5. strong baselines explain the same effect;
6. candidates fail intervention validation;
7. candidates fail transfer;
8. false candidates cannot be rejected;
9. the effect disappears under leakage controls;
10. results are not reproducible;
11. the benchmark unintentionally exposes ground truth;
12. the system requires post-hoc evaluation changes to succeed.

A negative result is scientifically valid.

# 33. S0.3 Research-Gap Verdict

**Current verdict: OPEN**

The proposed research gap has **not** been proven.

The current literature does not justify a broad claim that UNKNOWN introduces concept discovery, representation discovery, theory revision, intervention-based validation, or autonomous scientific discovery.

The research direction remains viable only if the project can establish a precise operational distinction between:

```text
Parameter Failure
        ↓
Model-Family Failure
        ↓
Representation Failure
```

and demonstrate that explicit representation-failure diagnosis contributes scientifically measurable value beyond:

* stronger model selection;
* unrestricted feature search;
* latent representation learning;
* theory revision;
* predicate/concept invention;
* always-on representation discovery.

The next stage must therefore focus on formal definitions and a benchmark capable of exposing these distinctions.
