# S0.5-D.5.1 — Baseline Representation Contract

**Project:** Project UNKNOWN — Autonomous Concept Formation & Model Discovery
**Stage:** S0.5-D.5.1
**Status:** Research specification
**Implementation status:** Not implemented

---

## 1. Purpose

This document defines the baseline representation `R₀` used by the controlled benchmark.

The purpose of `R₀` is to establish an explicit representational boundary against which parameter failure, model-family failure, and representation-level inadequacy can be distinguished.

`R₀` is not defined by the current capabilities or performance of a particular learning algorithm. It is a declared representational language consisting of public primitives, types, permitted operators, and composition constraints.

The benchmark must determine whether a required structure is expressible under this language before treating failure as evidence of representation-level inadequacy.

---

## 2. Scientific Principle

Prediction failure alone is insufficient evidence for representation failure.

A failure may result from:

1. incorrect parameters;
2. an inadequate model family;
3. stochastic variation;
4. insufficient evidence;
5. an inadequate representation.

Therefore the benchmark must establish representational sufficiency independently from current parameter values and selected model-family performance.

---

## 3. Formal Definition

Define the baseline representation language as:

`R₀ = (V₀, T₀, O₀, C₀)`

where:

- `V₀` — primitive vocabulary;
- `T₀` — type system;
- `O₀` — permitted representation operators;
- `C₀` — composition constraints.

A representation generated under these components is considered part of the baseline representation space.

The baseline is intentionally finite and explicitly declared.

It is not equivalent to unrestricted computation over raw observations.

---

## 4. Public Information Boundary

Only information available through the authoritative public environment interface may enter `R₀`.

Permitted information may include:

- public observations;
- observable entity properties;
- observable positions and relations;
- action information;
- intervention information;
- observed action outcomes;
- observed intervention outcomes;
- explicitly permitted temporal information.

The representation must not directly contain:

- hidden world state;
- hidden environment parameters;
- evaluator annotations;
- condition identifiers;
- representation-sufficiency labels;
- model-family labels;
- private mechanism identifiers;
- recommended candidate extensions;
- future observations;
- future outcomes.

UNKNOWN receives evidence, not evaluator conclusions.

---

## 5. Primitive Vocabulary

The initial vocabulary is organized by typed categories.

### 5.1 Entity

Represents an observable entity participating in the public environment.

An entity may contain:

- neutral identity;
- observable attributes;
- observable state.

Entity identifiers must not encode semantic roles or benchmark conditions.

Examples:

`entity_0`

`entity_1`

`entity_2`

Semantic identifiers such as `predator`, `cause`, `target`, or `leader` are not permitted unless independently justified as public environmental information.

### 5.2 Scalar

Finite numeric quantities exposed by the public interface.

Examples include:

- scalar measurements;
- distances;
- timestamps where explicitly exposed;
- scalar action parameters.

### 5.3 Vector

Typed finite-dimensional quantities exposed by the public interface.

Examples include:

- position;
- velocity;
- displacement.

### 5.4 Boolean

Explicit observable true/false properties.

### 5.5 Categorical

Explicitly observable categorical values.

Categories must not encode hidden evaluator semantics.

### 5.6 Relation

Explicitly observable relationships between entities or other permitted objects.

A relation is baseline information only when it is observable or explicitly defined as a permitted baseline derivation.

### 5.7 Time

Explicit temporal information permitted by the environment contract.

Examples may include:

- current step index;
- bounded previous-state references;
- explicitly permitted temporal differences.

### 5.8 Action

The ordinary action selected by UNKNOWN and the public information associated with that action.

### 5.9 Intervention

The intervention selected by UNKNOWN and the public information associated with that intervention.

Interventions remain semantically distinct from ordinary actions.

---

## 6. Representation Operators

`R₀` uses a finite, explicitly documented operator set.

Candidate baseline operators include:

- comparison;
- arithmetic difference;
- bounded arithmetic transformation;
- distance;
- aggregation;
- selection;
- pairwise relation construction;
- bounded temporal difference.

Each operator must have:

1. a declared input type;
2. a declared output type;
3. explicit semantics;
4. deterministic behavior where required;
5. documented composition constraints.

No unrestricted arbitrary-function operator is part of `R₀`.

---

## 7. Composition Constraints

Operators may only be composed when their type signatures permit the composition.

For example:

`Vector × Vector → Scalar`

may define a permitted distance operation.

A representation is therefore generated through valid typed compositions rather than unrestricted computation.

The benchmark must maintain an explicit record of the operations available in `R₀`.

This prevents a representation failure from being defined against an undefined or effectively universal baseline language.

---

## 8. Explicit Exclusion Boundary

The following are not baseline representation primitives unless a future benchmark specification explicitly justifies them as public information:

- latent variables;
- hidden mechanisms;
- causal roles;
- semantic abstractions;
- unobserved state;
- evaluator labels;
- condition labels;
- world-family labels;
- candidate representations;
- future observations;
- future outcomes;
- unrestricted learned abstractions.

The exclusion boundary prevents the benchmark from embedding the target discovery problem into the baseline representation.

---

## 9. Derived Structure vs. Discovered Abstraction

A distinction must be maintained between:

1. primitive information;
2. permitted derived structure;
3. newly constructed representation.

For example:

`position(A)`

and

`position(B)`

may permit a declared baseline operator:

`distance(A,B)`

If distance is part of `O₀`, it remains within `R₀`.

A later construct such as:

`persistent_interaction_state(A,B)`

is not automatically part of `R₀`.

Its status depends on whether it is explicitly present in `V₀` or constructible through `O₀` and `C₀`.

This distinction is central to representation-level discovery.

---

## 10. Representation Sufficiency

A required structure `Q` is representation-sufficient under `R₀` when `Q` is expressible within the declared representation language:

`Q ∈ Expressible(R₀)`

Expressibility must be evaluated independently of the current parameter values of UNKNOWN.

It must also be distinguished from the capacity of a particular model family.

Therefore:

> Failure of one model to recover `Q` does not establish that `Q` is outside `R₀`.

---

## 11. Representation-Level Inadequacy

A condition may qualify as representation-level inadequacy only when the required structure is not expressible under `R₀` and the benchmark independently establishes that parameter optimization and the permitted model-family alternatives cannot repair the limitation.

Conceptually:

`Q ∉ Expressible(R₀)`

while:

`Q ∈ Expressible(R₁)`

for a valid representation extension `R₁`.

The benchmark must also establish that the extension has measurable scientific consequences under the predefined evaluation protocol.

Performance improvement is evidence supporting a representation hypothesis; it is not the definition of representation failure.

---

## 12. Representation Extension

A representation extension has the form:

`R₁ = Extend(R₀, c)`

where `c` is a candidate representational construct.

A candidate must eventually have documented:

- candidate identifier;
- parent representation;
- primitive components;
- construction operation;
- generation context;
- supporting evidence;
- predictions;
- intervention predictions;
- falsification conditions;
- complexity characteristics.

A candidate is not accepted solely because it improves one metric on one environment.

---

## 13. Representation Equivalence

Novelty must be evaluated structurally rather than by string identity.

Two candidate representations with different names may nevertheless be equivalent if they encode materially equivalent information and produce equivalent relevant behavior under the benchmark's equivalence protocol.

Therefore:

> A new name does not constitute a new representation.

The benchmark must distinguish:

- renaming;
- reparameterization;
- exact reuse;
- structurally equivalent reconstruction;
- genuinely new composition.

Formal equivalence testing will be specified in a later stage.

---

## 14. Leakage Constraints

The baseline representation must not expose information that identifies the benchmark's hidden condition.

The following leakage classes remain prohibited:

- condition metadata;
- hidden mechanism identifiers;
- representation-status flags;
- evaluator explanations;
- canonical candidate names;
- private seed information;
- filesystem artifacts;
- exception-message leakage;
- timing-based condition leakage.

Identity and ordering must not provide unintended semantic information.

---

## 15. Scientific Invariants

The baseline representation contract must satisfy:

1. Public information only.
2. Explicit finite vocabulary.
3. Explicit type system.
4. Explicit operator set.
5. Explicit composition constraints.
6. No unrestricted computation primitive.
7. No hidden evaluator information.
8. No predefined target abstraction.
9. Model-family failure remains distinguishable from representation failure.
10. Parameter failure remains distinguishable from representation failure.
11. Representation novelty is structural rather than lexical.
12. Extensions remain experimentally falsifiable.

---

## 16. Open Questions

The following remain implementation/design questions rather than unresolved principles:

- exact primitive type definitions;
- exact operator signatures;
- maximum composition depth;
- whether bounded temporal history belongs directly in `R₀`;
- representation serialization format;
- formal expressibility checking;
- structural equivalence testing;
- extension complexity measurement;
- candidate execution safety;
- benchmark-specific representation languages.

These questions will be resolved in later D.5 stages.

---

## 17. Scientific Review

### Review question

Can `R₀` provide a stable and explicit boundary for distinguishing representation inadequacy from parameter and model-family failure?

### Current conclusion

Yes, provided that the baseline language remains:

- finite;
- typed;
- explicitly documented;
- independently justified;
- isolated from evaluator-private information;
- free from unrestricted arbitrary-function composition.

The baseline representation language must not be weakened merely to manufacture representation-failure cases.

Conversely, it must not be made so expressive that every possible abstraction is already constructible.

The correct benchmark boundary is therefore a scientifically justified representation language whose expressiveness is declared before evaluation.

---

## 18. D.5.1 Decision

**Decision:** Adopt a finite, typed, explicitly declared baseline representation language for `R₀`.

**Status:** Research decision accepted.

**Implementation:** Deferred.

**Next research dependency:** Define concrete world families and construct controlled R0/P1/M1/R1/N1/X1 conditions against this representation contract.
