# Project UNKNOWN — S0.3 Representation Specification

**Status:** Working scientific specification
**Sprint:** Sprint 0 — S0.3
**Date:** 2026-09-23
**Purpose:** Define the representation-level objects, failure categories, candidate formation constraints, and acceptance semantics used by Project UNKNOWN.

---

# 1. Purpose

This document defines the operational representation framework for Project UNKNOWN.

The purpose is to prevent the system from treating every prediction failure as a representation problem and to prevent arbitrary feature engineering from being treated as concept discovery.

The specification is intentionally bounded.

Project UNKNOWN does not claim to determine absolute information-theoretic representation sufficiency over every possible model or representation language.

Instead, it evaluates representation adequacy relative to:

1. a declared observation interface;
2. a declared representation language;
3. a declared model-family set;
4. a declared complexity/search budget;
5. a frozen evaluation protocol.

---

# 2. Core Objects

Let the environment be represented as:

$$
E=(S,A,O,T)
$$

where:

* \(S\) = hidden environment state;
* \(A\) = available actions/interventions;
* \(O\) = observation process;
* \(T\) = environment transition or data-generating mechanism.

UNKNOWN does not directly observe \(S\).

It receives observations generated through \(O\).

---

# 3. Observation History

At time \(t\), UNKNOWN receives an observation history:

$$
o_{\leq t}=(o_1,\ldots,o_t)
$$

The system may construct a representation:

$$
z_t=R(o_{\leq t})
$$

where \(R\) is the current representation.

---

# 4. Representation

A representation is a transformation from permitted observations and permitted observation history into a structured state used by downstream models.

$$
R:O_{\leq t}\rightarrow Z_t
$$

A representation may contain:

* scalar values;
* categorical values;
* relational variables;
* temporal variables;
* symbolic predicates;
* structured objects;
* bounded compositions of permitted primitives.

A representation is not assumed to be neural, symbolic, continuous, discrete, or otherwise tied to one implementation technology.

---

# 5. Model Family

Given representation \(R\), UNKNOWN operates against a predefined admissible model-family set:

$$
\mathcal{M}_R
$$

The model family is fixed by the experimental protocol before final evaluation.

A model-family member may include:

* parameters;
* architecture choices permitted by the protocol;
* optimization procedure;
* explicitly permitted inductive biases.

UNKNOWN must not declare representation failure solely because one weak model fails.

---

# 6. Parameter Failure

Parameter failure occurs when the current representation and an admissible model family can support the required behavior, but the selected parameters are inadequate.

Conceptually:

$$
R\text{ adequate}
$$

$$
M\in\mathcal{M}_R\text{ adequate}
$$

but:

$$
\theta\text{ inadequate}
$$

The expected remedy is parameter optimization, retraining, or recalibration.

Parameter failure must be resolved or reasonably ruled out before representation failure is declared.

---

# 7. Model-Family Failure

Model-family failure occurs when the current representation is sufficient for the task but the currently selected model family cannot express the required behavior.

Evidence requires at least one controlled alternative model family capable of meeting the predefined adequacy criterion while using the same representation.

Formally:

$$
\exists M_1\in\mathcal{M}_R:
Score(M_1,R)\geq\tau
$$

while the currently selected model family fails.

The expected remedy is model-family expansion or replacement.

---

# 8. Representation Failure

Representation-level inadequacy is a bounded scientific hypothesis.

Let:

$$
\mathcal{M}_R
$$

be the predefined admissible model family under representation \(R\).

Representation failure is supported when:

$$
\forall M\in\mathcal{M}_R:
Score(M,R)<\tau
$$

after the predefined optimization and model-family controls have been exhausted,

and an admissible extension \(R'\) permits:

$$
\exists M'\in\mathcal{M}_{R'}:
Score(M',R')\geq\tau
$$

This condition is necessary but not sufficient for discovery.

Improved prediction alone does not establish concept discovery.

---

# 9. Language Insufficiency

A separate failure category is required when the representation-extension language cannot express the required distinction.

Let:

$$
\mathcal{L}_{ext}
$$

be the predefined representation-extension language.

If the required representation is outside the expressible search space:

$$
C^*\notin\mathcal{L}_{ext}
$$

then failure to construct \(C^*\) must not automatically be classified as a failure of the discovery mechanism.

The experiment must distinguish:

1. expressible but undiscovered candidate;
2. candidate excluded by the representation language;
3. candidate excluded by search budget;
4. candidate rejected by validation.

---

# 10. Representation Language

The representation language is defined before the discovery experiment.

It consists of:

## 10.1 Observation primitives

Operations directly supported by the observation interface.

Examples may include:

* object attributes;
* measured quantities;
* permitted temporal observations.

## 10.2 Comparison primitives

Examples:

* equality;
* inequality;
* ordering;
* difference.

## 10.3 Relational primitives

Examples:

* distance;
* relative position;
* overlap;
* permitted object-to-object relations.

## 10.4 Temporal primitives

Examples:

* change;
* persistence;
* ordering;
* bounded temporal aggregation.

## 10.5 Logical composition

Examples:

* conjunction;
* disjunction;
* negation.

The exact primitive inventory is environment-specific but must be frozen before the final experiment.

---

# 11. Extension Language

The extension language defines what UNKNOWN is permitted to construct.

A candidate must satisfy:

$$
C\in\mathcal{L}_{ext}
$$

and:

$$
Complexity(C)\leq B
$$

where \(B\) is the declared search/complexity budget.

UNKNOWN may not introduce arbitrary executable code, hidden simulator variables, test-set information, or unrestricted external functions.

---

# 12. Primitive Versus Discovered Concept

A primitive operation is not itself a discovered concept.

For example:

$$
distance(A,B)
$$

may be available as a primitive.

A candidate concept may instead be:

$$
near(A,B)
=
distance(A,B)<\delta
$$

The discovery claim concerns the candidate and its scientific validation, not merely the availability of the primitive.

---

# 13. Candidate Representation

A candidate representation extension is:

$$
R'=R\oplus C
$$

where \(C\) is generated from the permitted extension language.

A candidate must include:

* formal expression;
* primitive dependencies;
* construction history;
* complexity;
* discovery trigger;
* hypothesis;
* predicted consequences;
* intervention predictions;
* falsification conditions.

---

# 14. Candidate Formation Trigger

Candidate generation should normally be triggered by systematic residual structure rather than arbitrary feature search.

A residual pattern is considered systematic only under a predefined analysis procedure.

Random or isolated errors must not automatically trigger representation extension.

The trigger procedure must be frozen before final evaluation.

---

# 15. Candidate Hypothesis

A candidate must be associated with a testable hypothesis.

The hypothesis must state:

1. what distinction the candidate is intended to represent;
2. what observations it predicts;
3. what intervention consequences are expected;
4. what observations would falsify it;
5. where the candidate is expected to remain valid.

A candidate without testable consequences is not sufficient for a discovery claim.

---

# 16. Candidate Validation

Candidate validation consists of multiple independent dimensions.

A candidate must be evaluated using:

1. prospective prediction;
2. intervention testing where feasible;
3. falsification testing;
4. unseen-context transfer;
5. complexity/search-budget compliance.

Prediction improvement alone is insufficient.

---

# 17. Candidate Rejection

A candidate must be rejected when it fails a predefined mandatory criterion.

Examples include:

* failure on held-out prediction;
* failure under intervention;
* failure of transfer;
* violation of complexity constraints;
* evidence of leakage;
* dependence on unavailable information;
* inability to reproduce the claimed result.

---

# 18. Candidate Revision

Revision is permitted only when the evaluation protocol explicitly allows another hypothesis-generation cycle.

Every revision must preserve:

* provenance;
* previous candidate;
* reason for revision;
* changed assumptions;
* new candidate definition.

Repeated modification until a candidate happens to pass is not acceptable without accounting for the search process.

---

# 19. Discovery and Validation Separation

Candidate construction must use discovery data.

Final validation must use data that were not used to construct or select the candidate.

The validation set must not influence:

* candidate generation;
* candidate ranking;
* representation grammar modification;
* stopping decisions.

---

# 20. Ground Truth

Synthetic environments may contain hidden ground-truth variables.

UNKNOWN must not receive those variables during discovery.

Ground truth is used only for:

* benchmark construction;
* post-hoc scientific analysis;
* diagnostic classification;
* measuring whether a candidate belongs to an intended equivalence class.

Exact ground-truth variable recovery is not required when an independently validated equivalent representation exists.

---

# 21. Representation Equivalence

Two representations \(R_1\) and \(R_2\) may be considered empirically equivalent relative to a frozen evaluation suite \(\mathcal{E}\):

$$
R_1\sim_{\mathcal{E}}R_2
$$

when they satisfy the predefined equivalence criteria across:

* prediction;
* intervention;
* falsification;
* transfer.

This does not imply universal mathematical equivalence.

It establishes empirical equivalence only within the declared evaluation regime.

---

# 22. Exact, Equivalent, and Predictive-Only Recovery

The benchmark distinguishes:

### Exact recovery

The candidate matches the predefined ground-truth representation.

### Equivalent recovery

The candidate differs structurally but satisfies the predefined empirical equivalence criteria.

### Predictive-only recovery

The candidate improves prediction but fails one or more independent scientific validation criteria.

Only exact or validated equivalent recovery may count toward a representation-discovery result.

---

# 23. Bounded Parsimony

Among candidates satisfying the mandatory scientific criteria, complexity may be used as a secondary selection criterion.

The objective is not to prove global minimality.

Instead:

$$
C^*=
\arg\min_C Complexity(C)
$$

subject to:

$$
Valid(C)=1
$$

within the declared search space and budget.

---

# 24. Counterfactual Failure Benchmark

The benchmark must include paired conditions.

## Condition A — Model limitation

The relevant information is present in the current representation, but the initial model family is insufficient.

A permitted stronger model should solve the task without representation extension.

Expected classification:

$$
Model\text{-}family\ failure
$$

## Condition B — Representation limitation

The required distinction is unavailable in the current representation, while an admissible representation extension can express it.

Expected classification:

$$
Representation\text{-}level\ inadequacy
$$

UNKNOWN must distinguish these cases without access to their hidden labels.

---

# 25. Encoded and Removed Conditions

Where practical, benchmark families should include:

### Explicit

The relevant information is directly represented.

### Encoded

The information is present but transformed or entangled.

### Removed

The information is unavailable from the current representation.

This prevents a simple decoder-failure test from being mistaken for representation diagnosis.

---

# 26. Decoy Candidates

Benchmark environments should contain opportunities for candidates that:

* correlate with the target;
* improve discovery-set prediction;
* fail under intervention;
* fail under transfer;
* exploit accidental regularities.

The system must reject these candidates.

---

# 27. Required Baselines

UNKNOWN must eventually be compared with at least:

1. fixed representation;
2. parameter optimization;
3. stronger model-family selection;
4. conventional feature discovery;
5. latent representation search;
6. predictive-only feature expansion;
7. always-on representation search.

The purpose is to determine whether explicit failure diagnosis contributes beyond unrestricted search or stronger modeling.

---

# 28. Central Ablation

The primary mechanism-level ablation is:

### UNKNOWN

$$
Failure\ Diagnosis
\rightarrow
Representation\ Extension
\rightarrow
Validation
$$

versus:

### Always-on Search

$$
Representation\ Search
\rightarrow
Validation
$$

The same relevant data and declared computational budget should be used where experimentally feasible.

---

# 29. Anti-Leakage Rules

UNKNOWN must not:

* access hidden state;
* access ground-truth concept names;
* inspect held-out transfer environments during discovery;
* use test outcomes to construct candidates;
* modify the representation language after seeing validation results;
* modify the evaluation criterion after seeing results;
* access hidden simulator APIs;
* receive human hints about the intended concept.

---

# 30. Discovery Claim

A representation discovery claim requires evidence that:

1. the current representation was subjected to the predefined adequacy procedure;
2. parameter failure was controlled;
3. model-family failure was controlled;
4. representation extension was permitted under a frozen language;
5. candidate construction used only permitted discovery information;
6. candidate validation was independent;
7. the candidate survived prediction, intervention, falsification, and transfer tests as applicable;
8. simpler alternative explanations were investigated;
9. the result is reproducible.

---

# 31. What Does Not Count

The following do not independently constitute concept discovery:

* lower training loss;
* higher validation accuracy alone;
* a new latent vector;
* a newly named variable;
* an arbitrary engineered feature;
* a larger neural network;
* a more expressive model;
* memorization;
* success only in the discovery environment;
* correlation without independent validation;
* exact recovery of a simulator variable without demonstrating useful scientific behavior.

---

# 32. Scientific Limitation

UNKNOWN cannot establish absolute representation insufficiency over all imaginable models.

All claims are relative to:

$$
(\mathcal{L}_{obs},
\mathcal{L}_{ext},
\mathcal{M},
B,
\mathcal{E})
$$

where:

* \(\mathcal{L}_{obs}\) = observation language;
* \(\mathcal{L}_{ext}\) = extension language;
* \(\mathcal{M}\) = admissible model families;
* \(B\) = declared computational/search budget;
* \(\mathcal{E}\) = frozen evaluation protocol.

This limitation is intentional and must be stated in experimental reporting.

---

# 33. Current Status

This specification is a working S0.3 scientific contract.

It is not yet an implementation architecture.

The next stage is to translate this contract into a concrete benchmark and evaluation protocol without allowing the benchmark designer to leak the intended answer.

**S0.3 remains open until that benchmark design survives adversarial review.**
